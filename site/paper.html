Institutional Determinants of Privacy Design in Central Bank Digital Currencies

Martin Bok & Dominik Stroukal · working paper, v11 · a measurement and data paper with a limited, pre-registered empirical illustration.

Abstract target abstract — results to be filled by the registered analysis

Trackers tell us whether a central bank is researching, piloting or has launched a retail CBDC; they do not tell us what it has committed to. We build an open dataset of documented privacy and identifiability design choices for every retail CBDC project with an official document (— projects, — documents, — countries), coded under a written measurement contract: for each project, whether the central bank can routinely link an online payment to a person (F1), whether the user's provider can (F2), whether a private offline mode is an adopted commitment (F4), and seven further cells on identification, re-identification, programmability, lawful access and ledger technology. Every value is tied to a verbatim quote, a page, a commitment status and a date, extracted by a reproducible two-provider pipeline with a mechanical citation check and audited against an independent human reference on a sealed test set. The three architecture cells define six nominal types; — projects receive a full type. Declared objectives are recorded as non-exclusive flags and described, not used to classify. As a pre-registered illustration we estimate, with a Firth logistic model and a smallest effect of interest fixed by elicitation, whether the size of the informal economy and the level of liberal democracy are associated with central-bank separation from transaction identity (Y₁). What the paper claims, and what it does not

It claims a validated instrument (definitions, inter-coder and model–human agreement, citation-verified provenance) and a documented map with a time dimension. It does not claim that institutions cause designs: associations are reported as "is associated with", on 20–50 determinable projects, with the smallest effect of interest fixed in advance, and whatever they show. Purposes are described, never used to rank countries. Earlier versions' vocabulary-based categories and privacy index are retired; the v1 release stays archived at cbdcdataset.org for the record.
