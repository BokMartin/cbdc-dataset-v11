Who can see a CBDC payment — and what did the central bank actually commit to?

Version 11 replaces the vocabulary-based purpose categories of the first release with a measured matrix: for each retail CBDC project, a small set of yes/no design questions (F1–F12) answered only from official documents, each answer tied to a verbatim quote, a page, a commitment status and a date. What changed since v1

From "what is it for" to "what did they commit to, and who can see what".

The first release counted design vocabulary and sorted projects into six purpose "centres". Four rounds of external review found the categories hard to validate and the privacy index sensitive to sample choice. v11 keeps the open, document-based idea but changes the measurement: every project gets a feature matrix answered from its own official documents under a written measurement contract. Absence of mention is unknown, never no; a tested capability is not an adopted commitment; a staff paper never establishes a commitment. Purposes survive as declared, non-exclusive objectives (F11) — described, not used to classify.

1

Architecture layer. Three questions decide the type: can the central bank routinely link an online payment to a person (F1), can the user's provider (F2), and is a private offline mode an adopted commitment (F4). Six cells A0 … C1; C is expected to be empty among adopted designs — stated in advance.

2

Time layer. Every cell carries the year and status of the document it comes from, so a project's trajectory (option → tested → adopted, or a reversal) is part of the data. The map has a year slider for exactly this.

3

Validation first. Extraction is a logged, reproducible API pipeline with a mechanical citation check; two authors verify development profiles; a sealed blind test set is coded by humans without model output; hypotheses and a smallest effect of interest are registered before any cell is computed.

Where this stands

Corpus and selection are frozen (core_v1, 18 Oct 2026, plus a dated UK supplement). The contract is at v1.1 after the first two worked profiles. Norway and the United Kingdom are coded as development profiles with verified quotes; the remaining 54 projects on the map are mock values generated to show the finished product. See Method & progress for the step-by-step plan.
