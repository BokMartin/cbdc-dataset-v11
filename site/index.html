What do central banks say about CBDC privacy, and what have they committed to?

An open dataset, in development, built only from official documents of the bodies that design retail central bank digital currencies. Every value will lead to a document, a page and a sentence. Two layers are kept apart: what a project says about users' privacy, and what it has committed to.

Explore the mapBrowse the documents
Preliminary results. The documents have been read by three language models and the results are shown here as they stand. The authors are verifying the cells against the sources and have not yet chosen how the three models are combined, so every number can still change and both readings are shown. Everything is open: the code, the decisions and the run records are in the repository.

What is here now

Privacy index

What the documents state about each design, as an interval from 0 to 1, with every cell traced to a quotation, and the hypothesis "much talk, few commitments".

Cell history

How each design question developed, document by document, and where documents reversed or contradicted one another.

Privacy paragraphs

The passages in which projects speak about users' privacy in their own voice, searchable, each linked to its page.

PETs

Privacy-enhancing technologies named in the design documents, coded with the list of Nijsse and Pinto (2026).

Map of the 44 projects

43 national retail CBDC projects and the digital euro, coloured by official status or by the official documents we read, year by year.

Every document

Which documents are evidence, which are cited as companions and which were not used, who decided, in which language, with links to the issuer and an archived copy.

How it is built

The source rule, the two layers, the checks, and what the dataset will not claim.

What comes next

The authors verify every value-bearing cell against the cited page and decide how the three models are combined; the numbers on this site are then replaced by the verified ones, with the same pages. The paper follows.
