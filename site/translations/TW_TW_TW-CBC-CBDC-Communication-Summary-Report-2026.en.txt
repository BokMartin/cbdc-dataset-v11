Abstract

  To keep pace with the digital era and international development trends, the Bank began research and experimentation on central bank digital currency (CBDC) in Year 108. For retail CBDC, after completing the experimental project, the Bank established a prototype platform (with functions including transfers, shopping payments, and support for digital voucher payments), and has continued to improve the platform's information security technology and processing performance. For wholesale CBDC, a tokenized funds-flow experimental platform has been established, and the feasibility of using wholesale CBDC to support the clearing of various tokenized asset transactions is currently being tested, in response to the development trend of tokenized assets.

  As CBDC involves a broad range of matters, and its related operations and mechanism design concern stakeholders such as financial institutions, payment businesses, enterprises, and the public, the Bank considers that, building on the current CBDC-related research and experimental projects, it should understand external needs and views regarding CBDC and increase awareness of CBDC across different sectors. Accordingly, extensive communication work has been carried out since Year 112, including a written questionnaire survey completed in Year 113 and face-to-face communication activities held in Year 114.

  Regarding face-to-face communication activities, in Year 114 the Bank held a total of 9 public hearings, briefing sessions, and forums in the northern, central, and southern regions, broadly inviting people from different sectors to participate, listening to their views and exchanging relevant information. Invitees included industry (such as financial institutions, electronic payment institutions, and virtual asset businesses), civil society organizations, government agencies, and academia, and were representative. The Bank has compiled in this report the topics of concern raised by different sectors during the 9 communication activities and the Bank's responses (see pages 13~32 for details).

  At present, the progress of CBDC development in our country is in step with that of major advanced countries, all of which continue to conduct research and experimentation steadily. Taiwan's existing payment system is diverse and convenient, and there is not yet an urgent need to issue retail CBDC. Therefore, funds-flow operations supporting the distribution of digital vouchers are being used first as a trial implementation item to support the government's various distribution policies. Meanwhile, the Bank continues research and experimentation on wholesale CBDC supporting tokenized assets, to ensure that central bank money continues to provide a foundation of trust for the future tokenized monetary system.

I. Background

     In response to developments in the digital era, the Bank began researching central bank digital currency (Central Bank Digital Currency, CBDC) in Year 108. CBDC is divided into retail CBDC and wholesale CBDC. Retail CBDC is cash in digital form for the public's everyday transactions; wholesale CBDC is tokenized central bank reserves, used to handle transfers of deposit tokens between financial institutions1 or the clearing of other token assets.

      To keep pace with international development trends and explore the feasibility and necessity of central bank issuance of CBDC, the Bank completed two stages of research and experimental projects in June of Year 109 and June of Year 111, respectively: the “Technical Research on the Feasibility of Wholesale CBDC” and the “Retail CBDC Experimental Project.” For retail CBDC, the Bank established a prototype platform and planned the related operational architecture, participating institutions, system security, and data protection [資料保護], and developed basic functions such as transfers, stored-value top-ups, and payments, as well as value-added functions such as digital voucher payments. As for wholesale CBDC, the recent emergence of tokenization technologies such as distributed ledger technology (DLT) [分散式帳本] and blockchain, combined with innovative applications such as smart contracts (Smart Contract), has given rise to many different tokenized assets. Their technological advantages have attracted international attention. In this regard, the Bank has also recently cooperated with Financial Information Service Co., Ltd., financial institutions, and the Taiwan Depository & Clearing Corporation, among other entities, to jointly explore the application potential of wholesale CBDC.

      As retail CBDC involves a broad range of matters, its related operations and mechanism design also concern stakeholders such as financial institutions, payment businesses, enterprises, and the public; wholesale CBDC may also affect the monetary or financial system as new technologies develop. On this basis, the Bank considers that it should, building on the current CBDC-related research and experimental projects, further understand external needs and views regarding CBDC, in order to increase awareness of CBDC across different sectors. Accordingly, the Bank began extensive communication work in Year 112, divided into a written questionnaire survey and face-to-face communication activities.

II. Status of Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC) Experimentation and Future Directions

(I) Retail CBDC

     After completing the retail CBDC experimental project, the Bank established a “CBDC prototype platform,” planned the related operational architecture, participating institutions, system security, and data protection, and developed functions such as transfers, stored-value top-ups, payments, and support for digital voucher payments2. These have been used as background information for external communication, and are briefly explained below:

1. Positioning and Core Principles

     Retail CBDC is digital cash. In response to development needs in the digital era, and to maintain the role of central bank money as a foundation of trust in the economic and financial system, the Bank adheres to the basic principles of “do no harm,” “coexistence,” and “innovation and efficiency”3 in its related planning and design.

2. Issuance through a Two-Tier Architecture [雙層式架構]

     To maintain the operating model of the traditional monetary system's two-tier architecture and avoid financial disintermediation (disintermediation), if a decision is made to issue CBDC in the future, a two-tier architecture will also be adopted. That is, the Bank will provide a CBDC platform, issue CBDC on the platform and allocate it to intermediaries [中介機構], which will then provide CBDC to users (Figure 1)4. Intermediaries are responsible for opening wallets [錢包] for users, providing wallet interfaces (such as mobile Apps), and other value-added application services.

    3. No Interest at the Initial Stage of Issuance, but Retaining the Interest-Bearing Function

        As deposits taken by banks and other intermediaries are a source of credit creation, if CBDC replaces bank deposits on a large scale, this could affect financial stability and, in turn, the effectiveness of monetary policy transmission. To prevent the public from converting large amounts of deposits into CBDC, if a decision is made to issue it in the future, it will initially be issued on a non-interest-bearing basis to maintain the existing monetary policy transmission mechanism; however, the interest-bearing function will be retained to preserve future policy flexibility.

5. Privacy [隱私] and Personal Data [個人資料] Protection

    Users opening an unnamed wallet need only provide a mobile phone number, without having to provide other personal data, which can also accommodate the public's privacy needs. When users open a named wallet, the intermediary conducts the “Know Your Customer” [認識你的客戶] (Know Your Customer, KYC) procedure, and the intermediary must properly keep and manage users' personal data in accordance with the Personal Data Protection Act and other provisions. It may consult [查閱] relevant data of its own users only within the scope authorized under the terms agreed between it and the users (or laws and regulations). Government agencies such as prosecutors and investigators may request access to [調閱] transaction records and user data from intermediaries only where lawful and necessary. As for the Bank, it may only consult transaction data processed using de-identification [去識別化] technology, and user information that must be retained for wallet opening (such as hash values of national identity card numbers and mobile phone numbers). It is difficult for the Bank to reconstruct or identify [識別] users' personal data.

6. Platform Functions

    In addition to supporting basic functions such as transfers between wallets, shopping payments, exchange (meaning mutual exchange between cash, bank deposits, and CBDC), and automatic conversion/top-up5, the platform can also support value-added functions such as digital voucher payments6.

    However, as payment instruments in our country are currently diverse and convenient, there is not yet an urgent need to issue retail CBDC. Therefore, the “CBDC prototype platform” is being used as the basis for establishing a “Digital Public Infrastructure Funds-Flow Platform” (abbreviated as the Digital Funds-Flow Platform), with support for digital voucher payment functions7 first being used as a trial implementation item, in order to verify the platform's technical feasibility and high-load capacity.

    Since August of Year 114, the Digital Funds-Flow Platform has handled funds-flow operations for the Hakka Affairs Council's Hakka Coin distribution project, and has operated smoothly to date. In November of the same year, it collaborated in the universal cash distribution of 1 ten-thousand dollars,

7. Information Security

       The platform's overall security adopts a defense in depth (defense in depth, DiD)9 architecture, with the Bank, intermediaries, and users jointly achieving security management objectives. Within this, the central bank maintains platform security; intermediaries establish information security management systems to ensure uninterrupted operations; and users comply with intermediaries' wallet management mechanisms. Transaction security uses a public-private key mechanism (Public Key Cryptography) to enhance transaction security and system resilience and security. In addition, the system platform has also introduced ISO standards to align the system with international standards in aspects such as related technologies (such as the deployment of firewalls and other information security equipment), operating procedures (such as permission management and abnormality alerts), and backup, in order to effectively improve system security.

(II) Wholesale CBDC

       In response to the development of Real World Asset (Real World Asset, RWA) tokenization, the latest survey by the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) shows10 that most central banks worldwide have already engaged in wholesale CBDC-related work. The main development directions include using wholesale CBDC to promote the development of cross-border payments and as a clearing instrument for tokenized assets.

       Regarding cross-border payments, although the BIS considers that CBDC may be one of the solutions for promoting cross-border payments, it also presents many challenges, including technological, legal and regulatory, policy, and governance issues. In this regard, the Bank continues to monitor international developments and actively participates in exchanges at relevant international conferences, to ensure that future development keeps pace internationally.

(I) CBDC’s positioning and policy objectives

Q1: What is CBDC’s positioning? Taiwan’s current payment system is already highly convenient; why does the Central Bank still need to research and test CBDC?

1. In response to national digital development policy and the public’s demand for digital payments, the Bank is advancing CBDC research and experimental plans. CBDC can be divided into retail and wholesale types. Retail CBDC is cash in digital form, for the public’s daily transactions; wholesale CBDC is tokenized reserves, used for clearing tokenized assets between financial institutions.

2. The Bank’s considerations for continuing research and testing are as follows:

(1) As regards retail CBDC, because Taiwan’s current payment system is comprehensive and its payment instruments are diverse and convenient (such as credit cards, debit cards, bank deposits and electronic payments),

payments can already be made immediately and at low cost, and therefore there is no urgency to issue it14, but research and testing are still necessary:

⚫ Retaining a public option: As the digital era develops, payment services provided by the private sector are becoming increasingly diverse and convenient. Even if payment services are provided entirely by the private sector in the future, the Central Bank still needs to retain the issuance of CBDC as a public option. This can effectively counterbalance potential private-sector monopolies and provide a safe, reliable means of payment outside private-sector payment systems, to safeguard the public’s rights and interests.

⚫ Achieving interoperability: To avoid fragmentation of the payment market and a lack of interoperability, which results in merchants needing to deploy multiple QR Codes or acquiring devices. CBDC’s objective is to establish a common infrastructure so that various payment instruments can all interoperate with CBDC and, on this common basis, allow private-sector businesses to compete fairly.

⚫ Serving as an efficient policy tool and supporting innovation:

– G2C or C2G applications: CBDC can serve as an efficient policy tool. For example, the Bank has already applied the CBDC prototype platform to support the payment functions of digital vouchers (such as Hakka coins), which can eliminate the printing and distribution of physical vouchers, effectively integrate the resources of relevant ministries, agencies and institutions, and precisely and directly provide the public with voucher collection and spending functions (Government to Citizen, G2C); conversely, in the future it could also enable the public to pay taxes and fees to the government (Citizen to Government, C2G), improving administrative efficiency.

– Filling market gaps: The existing payment market is driven by commercial interests and often overlooks less profitable application settings (such as government fees and medical institutions). CBDC can proceed from the perspective of the “public interest” to fill these market gaps and deepen financial inclusion.

Q2: Will the issuance of CBDC affect the monetary aggregate M2 or cause inflation?

      Like physical New Taiwan dollars, CBDC must be fully backed by issuance reserves (such as gold and foreign exchange reserves), and must be converted at an equivalent value of 1:1 with existing money. Therefore, the issuance of CBDC is merely a conversion between forms of money, will not increase the supply of the monetary aggregate M2, and poses no concern about affecting inflation:

1. Retail CBDC: Members of the public exchange physical cash (deposited through an ATM) or bank deposits (converted through an App) for it at equivalent value. If exchanged using cash, this is a change in the form of central bank money, and M2 remains unchanged; if exchanged using deposits, the components of M2 change (deposits decrease and central bank money increases), but the quantity of M2 remains unchanged.

2. Wholesale CBDC: Banks exchange reserves deposited with the central bank for it at equivalent value, which also does not affect the quantity of M215.

Q3: If CBDC is issued, will it impact the banking system and cause financial disintermediation? How will the central bank control this risk?

1. To avoid financial disintermediation caused by CBDC, the Bank sets a holding limit [持有餘額限制] on CBDC and does not pay interest on it, to avoid impacting the banking system:

     (1) No interest in the initial implementation period: CBDC does not pay interest, which can reduce the public's incentive to use CBDC as a store of value (compared with interest-bearing bank deposits), returning it to its essential nature as a payment tool.

     (2) Holding limit: An upper limit on holding balances is set for individuals and legal persons, to prevent the public from holding large amounts of CBDC and thereby causing an outflow of bank deposits.

Q2: What is the relationship between CBDC and private-sector money such as stablecoins and deposit tokens?

  CBDC, stablecoins, and deposit tokens can coexist and complement one another at different tiers [層級]:

1. The goal of retail CBDC is to establish a common infrastructure that can interoperate with other payment tools (such as deposit tokens and stablecoins), allowing the public to choose different payment methods according to their needs. In addition, to avoid impacting the banking system and causing financial disintermediation, retail CBDC may be non-interest-bearing, and wallet balances are subject to an upper limit; issuance adopts a two-tier architecture [雙層式架構] to maintain the operation of the existing monetary and financial systems.

2. Wholesale CBDC serves as a cross-system, cross-institution, cross-platform final clearing tool, supporting interoperability between different deposit tokens or stablecoins, to prevent fragmentation in the RWA market arising from insufficient interoperability between platforms.

(III) Legal Basis and Regulatory Framework

Q1: What is the legal basis for issuing CBDC? Does the central bank lean toward enacting a dedicated law or amending the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) Act?

1. Although Article 13 of the current Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) Act authorizes the Bank to issue national currency, there are conflicts with other provisions. For example, Article 15 of the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) Act uses terms such as the printing, minting, denominations, and forms of currency, which clearly concern physical currency and cannot fully cover CBDC in digital form. Therefore, the current legal basis for issuing CBDC appears insufficient.

2. Considering the economic benefits of legislation and the completeness of regulations, if CBDC is to be issued, the Bank may lean more toward enacting a dedicated law rather than merely amending the Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) Act:

(1) Basis for issuance and legal status: A dedicated law can comprehensively regulate CBDC's status as fiat currency, legal nature, and legal tender [法償] effect.

(2) Management of intermediaries [中介機構]: A regulatory framework for intermediaries needs to be established, clearly specifying their qualifications and conditions, as well as the rights and obligations between the Bank and intermediaries.
                20
=== PAGE 25 ===
(3) Civil law issues: CBDC may constitute an “intangible property right.” The effect and timing of the transfer of its property rights, as well as its legal status in bankruptcy or compulsory enforcement, all need to be clearly specified by law.

     (4) Criminal law issues: The types of crime faced by CBDC in digital form (such as tampering with the ledger [帳本] and system attacks) are entirely different from counterfeiting physical currency. The offense of currency counterfeiting under the current Criminal Code is difficult to apply to these, and a dedicated law is needed to address them.

3. Before CBDC is formally issued in the future, the relevant legal framework planning must first be completed, communication with the Ministry of Justice and other relevant agencies must take place, and relevant supporting measures must be formulated.

Q2: Under the two-tier architecture, what are the qualifications for becoming an intermediary? What are the legal relationship and supervisory model between the central bank and intermediaries?

1. Regarding qualifications for intermediaries, these could be banks or electronic payment institutions, as these institutions already have mature regulatory frameworks for operations, legal compliance, and information security, and are all suitable intermediaries.

2. Intermediaries will bear anti-money laundering (Anti-Money Laundering, AML) and KYC responsibilities, serving as the first line of defense in implementing anti-money laundering and fraud detection, and will only be able to view transaction information relating to their customers, to protect users' privacy [隱私].

3. The legal relationship between the Bank and intermediaries is currently still being formulated, and may be established through regulatory orders, contracts, or a combination of regulations and contracts.

(IV) System Architecture and Choice of Technical Solutions

Q1: Under current plans, why does retail CBDC use centralized technology, while wholesale CBDC uses distributed ledger technology (DLT) [分散式帳本技術]?

(V) Risk Management, Security, and Privacy Protection

Q2: As a digital currency, how does CBDC guard against information security risks (such as hacking, impersonation or data leaks)?

1. All CBDC transactions must involve members of the public signing with private keys and the platform verifying the signatures to ensure the expression of their intent and prevent impersonation.

2. The CBDC platform follows the requirements below to ensure security:

(1) The three elements of information security (CIA) proposed by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) of the United States: confidentiality [機密性] (C), integrity (I), and availability (A).

(2) System development and environment establishment are carried out according to the highest-level requirements of the Ministry of Digital Affairs' “Information and Communications System Protection Baselines.”

(3) It follows international standards such as ISO 27001 (information security management), ISO 27701 (privacy protection management [隱私保護管理]), and ISO 22301 (business continuity management), and regularly commissions an impartial third-party organization to conduct audits each year and obtains certification.

3. Measures taken by the CBDC platform to ensure security:

(1) Externally, a traffic-scrubbing mechanism from telecommunications operators is introduced, which can effectively guard against hackers' DDoS distributed denial-of-service attacks.

(2) Internally, a defense-in-depth architecture is adopted. In addition to adopting a whitelist mechanism to control legitimate sources, it also uses

(3) The platform has an endpoint detection and response mechanism that can automatically monitor abnormal behavior and issue timely warnings to prevent hackers from intruding and stealing data.

(5) Financial information security joint-defense intelligence messages from the Financial Information Sharing and Analysis Center (F-ISAC) are continuously received to quickly grasp the latest vulnerability threats, attack methods, scope of impact and response measures.

Q4: How does CBDC balance the tracking of transaction fund flows with transaction privacy [隱私]? Will the central bank arbitrarily monitor the public's transactions?

CBDC uses a data governance mechanism under a “two-tier architecture [雙層式架構]” to safeguard the privacy of the public's lawful transactions:

1. Privacy design:

(1) The CBDC platform does not retain customers' direct personal data [直接個資]. Personal data [個人資料] (data subjected to KYC by intermediaries) are retained only by intermediaries. All data transmitted to the CBDC platform are necessary for risk control and are processed using de-identification [去識別化] technology. The central bank cannot identify individuals and would find it difficult to restore the data, so as to protect personal privacy and reduce the risk of personal data leaks.

(2) Unregistered wallets set small-value thresholds, such as 1,000 US dollars or 3 ten-thousand New Taiwan dollars or less, with reference to the transaction limits [交易限額] of international anti-money laundering organizations (such as FATF) and the EU's digital euro. Members of the public who need only small-value payments need only confirm the corresponding relationship of the “mobile phone number” through telecommunications company certification (TWCA). It will not be directly linked to the holder's identity, thereby accommodating both the public's small-value payment needs and their privacy needs.

2. Tracking transaction fund flows: The Bank will not conduct routine checks. Only when abnormal transactions are discovered, or when prosecutorial, investigative or tax authorities comply with laws such as the Personal Data Protection Act and the Code of Criminal Procedure, and subject to explicit provisions in any dedicated legislation that may exist in the future, may data be requested from intermediaries according to statutory procedures and transaction records (excluding personal data) be requested from the Bank for comparison. In addition, the strength of data protection for CBDC holders will in no case be lower than the protection standards for financial consumers under current laws and regulations.

Q5: If a user's mobile phone is lost or malfunctions, can the CBDC in it be recovered?

Whether CBDC in a lost or malfunctioning mobile phone can be recovered depends on the type of wallet:

(6) Financial inclusion and public communication

Q1: How does CBDC implement financial inclusion and assist groups affected by the digital divide?

The Bank attaches great importance to financial inclusion and regards it as one of CBDC's core values:

1. Taking care of small minority groups: The existing electronic payment market is driven by commercial interests and may overlook less profitable small minority groups. CBDC starts from the public interest. As more and more merchants introduce CBDC in the future, financial inclusion can be expected to be strengthened.

2. A CBDC wallet can be opened without a bank account: Even if members of the public do not have a bank account, they can still open a CBDC wallet. Registered wallets must undergo KYC by an intermediary, while for unregistered wallets it is sufficient to provide a mobile phone number and verify its validity.

3. Studying the feasibility of hardware devices other than mobile phones: Mobile phones are the main medium for CBDC payments. Whether to provide payment devices other than mobile phones, such as physical cards, for use by members of the public who do not use mobile phones (such as some older people) will be studied and discussed.

4. The Bank will continue to provide cash: If CBDC is to be issued in the future, the Bank will still issue physical cash. That is, physical cash will not disappear but will coexist with CBDC, to ensure that groups that do not use digital payment instruments (such as some older people) can still use cash for transactions. This not only takes care of older people or people in remote areas and ensures financial inclusion, but also serves as a backup instrument when systemic risks occur in electronic payments or digital payments, helping the resilience of the payment environment.

Q2: What supporting measures are there for specific groups, such as people with disabilities or migrant workers?

1. Accessibility and ease of use will be strengthened in user interface or operational design:

(1) Intermediaries' wallet Apps must pass the Ministry of Digital Affairs' “accessibility testing (WCAG)” to ensure that groups such as people with visual impairments can easily carry out transactions.

(2) The user interface (UI) and user experience (UX) developed and designed by intermediaries must take into account the needs of different groups. For example, an operating mode with large fonts and simplified operation should be developed for older people.

(3) Intermediaries may assess the provision of diverse forms of outreach, such as developing “easy-to-read versions” of relevant teaching materials, adding sign-language interpretation, and establishing multiple consultation channels (such as sign-language video services), to help the public become familiar with using CBDC.

2. Migrant workers currently face problems in cross-border remittances such as relatively high barriers to obtaining financial services and bank business hours that do not meet their needs. In the future, CBDC could be used for cross-border payments. However, there are currently no common international standards for CBDC. Common standards for cross-border payment applications must first be established before they can actually be put into use. The BIS considers cross-border interlinking of countries' existing Fast Payment Systems (FPS) to be a goal more likely to be achieved first in the short term. The Bank will continue to closely follow international developments concerning cross-border payments to facilitate alignment with international practices.

3. In addition, to safeguard the rights and interests of older or disadvantaged people in using banknotes, even if the Bank decides to issue retail CBDC, physical cash will continue to be provided, and the feasibility of issuing physical cards will be studied.

CBDC is currently still at the research and experimentation stage, and there is no timetable for issuance yet. (7) Cross-border payments and real-world asset (RWA) tokenization applications

Q1: Can CBDC be used to improve the efficiency of cross-border payments?

CBDC is one of the possible improvement options for facilitating cross-border payments, but cross-border interlinking of countries' existing FPS is a more feasible improvement option in the short term:

1. CBDC cross-border payments still present challenges, including issues involving technology, laws and regulations, policy and governance that remain to be resolved. Therefore, countries' CBDCs are still undergoing research and experimentation.

2. The BIS considers FPS cross-border interlinking to be a potentially more feasible option for improving cross-border payments in the near term, and has therefore listed it as a priority initiative, hoping that countries' FPS will adopt consistent data exchange and communications standards. Our country's interbank financial information system is an FPS. In this regard, the Bank continues to participate in exchanges at relevant meetings of international financial organizations and urges the Financial Information Service Co., Ltd. to make preparations to facilitate future alignment with international systems.

Q2: What is the positioning of wholesale CBDC? What is its relationship with RWA tokens? What is the current progress of experimentation?

1. Wholesale CBDC is tokenized central bank reserves. It can serve as a settlement instrument for handling interbank transfers of deposit tokens or other asset tokens, thereby supporting innovative applications in financial markets, including the development of RWA tokens.

2. Since the end of year 113, the Bank has established a “tokenized fund-flow experimentation platform” and completed proofs of concept for deposit tokens, delivery versus payment (DvP), and special-purpose tokens (such as bid-bond tokens)27. In year 114, it cooperated with the Taiwan Depository & Clearing Corporation to complete a DvP experiment for corporate bond tokens. In addition, the Financial Supervisory Commission is also bringing together securities-related institutions and financial institutions to establish an RWA tokenization platform. However, the world of tokenized assets still needs tokenized money as a transaction instrument. If settlement (clearing) across institutions, systems or platforms is involved, wholesale CBDC is still needed as the final settlement instrument.

3. To avoid fragmented development of tokenization platforms, the Bank will subsequently continue to cooperate with the Taiwan Depository & Clearing Corporation in experimentation, exploring the feasibility of jointly building tokenized financial market infrastructure.
