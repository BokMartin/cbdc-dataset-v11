Chair, Committee Members, Ladies and Gentlemen:

It is a great honor to have been invited to your Committee today to report on the topic of “Planning for the Issuance of Digital Currency and the Digital Transformation of Money.” The report on this topic is respectfully presented below, and your guidance is welcome.

The modern monetary system consists primarily of central bank money (Central Bank Money) and commercial bank money (Commercial Bank Money), forming a two-tier system structure [雙層體系架構] in which the central bank and commercial banks operate in connection with one another (Figure 1). Central bank money is not only the anchor (Anchor)1 of the monetary system and the best settlement asset (Settlement Asset)2 for financial transactions, but also the foundation for maintaining financial stability and the effectiveness of monetary policy3.

In addition, the central bank also proactively responds to market funding conditions by adjusting the quantity of central bank money through open market operations, thereby influencing the financial system’s funding positions and ensuring the effectiveness of monetary policy implementation.

In response to the evolution of digital technologies4, the Bank is promoting the digital transformation of money and conducting research and experiments on central bank digital currency (CBDC), in the hope that the issuance of CBDC in the future can play a role and function in stabilizing money and finance. The current progress and future plans are described below:

I. Current progress:

(1) A CBDC prototype platform has been built

In response to digital developments in retail payments, the operating structure and functions5 of a retail (Retail) CBDC have been designed (Figure 2), and a prototype platform has been built. In the future, digital cash can be issued to provide the public with a means for everyday payments.

Note: The “CBDC dedicated vault,” “intermediary [中介機構] general vault,” and “user wallet [錢包]” in the platform system are wallets belonging respectively to the central bank, intermediaries, and end users, recording the amount of CBDC held.

Notes: 1. Explanation of the operating process: At an intermediary’s request, the central bank converts reserves into CBDC in the Central Bank Interbank Funds Transfer System and transfers them into the dedicated CBDC account [專戶]. At the same time, the platform credits an equivalent amount of CBDC to the CBDC dedicated vault, and then allocates it to the intermediary general vault; at a user’s request to convert deposits into CBDC, the intermediary provides CBDC to the user’s wallet.

1. Issuance using a two-tier structure [雙層式架構]: To avoid affecting the financial intermediation function, a two-tier structure is adopted, under which the Bank issues CBDC on the CBDC platform and allocates it to intermediaries (such as banks and electronic payment institutions), and the intermediaries then provide CBDC to users.

2. No interest in the initial stage of issuance, but the interest-bearing function is retained: To prevent the public from converting large amounts of deposits into CBDC, if a decision is made in the future to issue it, it will be treated as digital cash in the initial stage and issued without interest; however, the interest-bearing function is retained to maintain future policy flexibility6.

3. Wallet tiers [錢包分級]: Based on risk management considerations and convenience for public use, CBDC wallets will be divided into unnamed wallets [不記名錢包] and named wallets [記名錢包]; the CBDC platform has wallet transaction limits [交易金額上限] and holding limits [持有金額上限]; intermediaries may also set their own limits based on their own risk considerations, but these must not exceed the limits set by the platform; in accordance with anti-money laundering requirements, the relevant amount limits for unnamed wallets will be lower than those for named wallets.

4. Privacy [隱私] and personal data [個人資料] protection: Users may choose to open named or unnamed wallets. For named wallets, intermediaries are required to perform customer due diligence (KYC), and to properly keep and manage users’ personal data in accordance with the existing provisions on data privacy protection. Intermediaries may only view the data of their own users; the Bank, for its part, uses de-identification [去識別化] technology to process the relevant transactions and does not retain users’ detailed data.

6
The payment of interest on CBDC could give rise to a new monetary policy operating instrument; that is, the central bank might be able to influence market interest rates by adjusting the CBDC interest rate. In addition, under severe deflation, it could serve as an instrument for breaking through the zero lower bound on interest rates.

2. Refining platform technology:

(1) Considering the digital divide, relevant experiments are currently being conducted to allow members of the public who do not use mobile phones to use cards for offline payments [離線支付]; these experiments include allowing the public to choose to use mobile phones or cards to make offline payments when there is no network connection.

Recently, with the development of tokenization technology, the topic of asset tokenization has attracted considerable attention. In a tokenized world, central bank money is still needed as the anchor of the monetary system and as a settlement asset, and to build, together with commercial bank money, a safe,

(1) Virtual assets such as Bitcoin and stablecoins are not part of the digital monetary system

Money should have the three basic functions of a medium of exchange, a unit of account, and a store of value. Bitcoin and stablecoins do not possess the above monetary functions; Bitcoin has no anchoring effect because of hype, speculation, and large price fluctuations (Figure 3). Although stablecoins are currently the main medium of exchange in virtual markets, stress events may still cause their exchange value to deviate from face value (Figure 4), and their prices cannot remain stable at all times12. Therefore, these virtual assets can only be considered risky assets arising from the development of digital technology, and are not money.

III. Conclusion

(I) Promoting CBDC is not an international competition:

CBDC does not confer an advantage simply by being first. For example, in countries that have already issued CBDC or are conducting pilots [試點]14, development to date has fallen short of expectations. Like advanced countries, our country has diverse and convenient payment options, and therefore takes a more cautious attitude toward issuing CBDC. The main expectation is that the introduction of CBDC can meet the public’s digital payment needs and the government’s digital policy development objectives, so as to deliver substantive benefits.

(II) In the course of research and experimentation, the Bank is also improving domestic payment efficiency and innovation:

Although the Bank currently has no timetable for issuing CBDC, in the course of continued research and experimentation it has already been improving payment system processing efficiency and innovative applications. For example, the CBDC prototype [雛型] platform developed for retail payments can already support the fund-flow operations of digital vouchers, and the number of transactions accepted per second (TPS) has increased to 20,000; in addition, a proof of concept for wholesale CBDC is being planned, using wholesale CBDC as the settlement asset for asset tokenisation and combining it with the functions of bank deposit tokens to jointly construct the future digital monetary system.
