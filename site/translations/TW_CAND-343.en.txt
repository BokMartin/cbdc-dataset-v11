II. Key points in CBDC development

To give everyone a comprehensive overview of CBDC, I will explain, in sequence, the role of central bank money, the potential benefits of CBDC, risks, and policy responses.

(1) The role of central bank money

1. Central bank money has no credit risk or liquidity risk and is the foundation for the sound operation of payment systems

Modern payment systems are based on two forms of central bank money: central bank reserves and cash. Reserves are the settlement asset for processing interbank payment transactions between financial institutions¹², while cash is currently the only physical money that the public can obtain directly.

Taking our country as an example, large-value transactions between financial institutions, such as interbank loans, bonds, bills, stocks, and foreign exchange, processed through the “Central Bank's Interbank Funds Transfer System¹³,” as well as various private-sector payments, such as ATM withdrawals and transfers, remittances, bill payments, tax payments, purchase debits, and corporate fund transfers, processed through the “Financial Information Service Co., Ltd. Financial Information System¹⁴,” all use the Bank's reserves for real-time interbank fund transfers and settlement. The “Central Bank's Interbank Funds Transfer System” and the “Financial Information Service Co., Ltd. Financial Information System” are critical infrastructure for promoting domestic financial development and maintaining financial stability. 2. Central bank money can be used to provide intraday liquidity and emergency liquidity

At present, to enable interbank transactions (whether large-value payments in financial markets or private-sector retail payments) to complete the payment and receipt of funds in real time on the same day, central banks in various countries allow a paying bank experiencing a temporary shortage of funds to obtain intraday liquidity from the central bank at any time (to be repaid at the end of the day), to ensure that every transaction is completed in real time. This intraday liquidity is also central bank money. At present, the maximum amount used intraday by financial institutions in our country is approximately 16 billion yuan.

In addition, if financial institutions encounter a financial crisis, the central bank may, in its role as “lender of last resort,” provide emergency liquidity through the provision of central bank money, preventing the risk of runs in the financial system from causing chain reactions that affect financial stability.

3. Ensuring the effectiveness of monetary policy implementation through adjustments to central bank money

In addition to meeting the payment needs of the financial system described above, central banks can also adjust the quantity of reserve money by injecting or withdrawing central bank money through open market operations, thereby influencing the overall level of funds and interest rates in financial markets and achieving the objectives of monetary policy implementation.

(2) Potential benefits of CBDC

1. Enabling the public to obtain safe digital currency

Cash is the only central bank money that the public can use and is a very safe payment option. Although our country has developed various electronic payment methods, such as debit cards, credit cards, stored-value cards, bank accounts, and electronic payment accounts, cash is still widely used by the public.

In recent years, with the promotion of electronic payments, the electronic payment ratio¹⁵ has risen year by year, from 40 % in 2017 to 60% in the 1st quarter of this year (2022). Although the rate of growth has not been as rapid as in countries such as Sweden, in response to future demand for the digitalization of cash, the public may wish to have the option of CBDC, with the central bank providing a safe, trusted digital payment instrument with no cost of use and no credit or liquidity risk.

(3) Potential risks of CBDC and policy responses

1. Financial intermediation function

Deposits in the banking system are the source of credit creation. CBDC may affect banks' existing payment business and cause bank deposit outflows. Therefore, at present, internationally, both countries that have issued CBDC and those studying it adopt the two-tier structure [雙層式架構] of traditional currency issuance, allowing banks to continue to play the role of financial intermediaries [金融中介].

In addition, if CBDC is designed to bear interest, it will also become a substitute for bank deposits. Once the total amount of deposits in the banking system decreases, banks' funding costs will increase, in turn raising borrowing costs for individuals, households, and enterprises. To avoid affecting banks' financial intermediation function, a non-interest-bearing design may be chosen for CBDC.

2. Financial stability

Central bank money is a risk-free asset. If it circulates widely after digitalization, during a financial crisis, CBDC will cause bank deposit outflows even if it does not bear interest, because digital runs proceed faster, exposing banks to more severe liquidity risk.

To prevent large-scale bank deposit outflows, controls may be imposed on users' CBDC transaction limits [交易量之上限] and total holding limits [持有總量之上限]. However, such controls may affect the public's demand for use. How to strike a balance between public demand and financial intermediation is an issue that requires prudent study for CBDC.

3. Monetary policy implementation

At present, open market operations conducted by central banks to influence the funding positions of the banking system and achieve monetary policy objectives, whether injecting or withdrawing central bank money, are already carried out digitally. Therefore, CBDC will not change the way central banks conduct operations.

However, if CBDC bears interest, there may be interaction effects with monetary policy implementation. For example, if the interest rate on CBDC is comparable to the returns on other safe assets, individuals or enterprises may reduce their investments in bank deposits, Treasury bills, or money market mutual funds and increase their CBDC holdings. Once public demand for CBDC fluctuates substantially under the influence of market factors, it will change the level of funds in the banking system, in turn affecting the implementation and operation of central bank monetary policy.

Interest-bearing CBDC has broad potential effects on banks' deposits and lending, households' and enterprises' investment decisions, and central banks' monetary policy implementation, and might serve as a new policy instrument. However, to avoid CBDC causing shocks to the financial system, even if CBDC has an interest-bearing design, in the initial stage of implementation it should first be treated as digital cash and issued on a non-interest-bearing basis. At present, countries such as mainland China and The Bahamas that are piloting or issuing CBDC have all stated that CBDC does not bear interest.

The financial transaction data generated by CBDC are generated in the same way as those currently produced through transactions via financial institutions. Because CBDC operates through a two-tier structure [雙層架構], with respect to privacy protec-

IV. Conclusion

The digital economy drives demand for digital currency, giving everyone unlimited scope for imagining digital currency. However, when actually exploring this new field, many theoretical ideas will face technical and practical tests and challenges.

First, in terms of “technology,” for example, if technologies and governance that tout decentralization still present numerous risks in operational performance, information security protection, and protection of users' rights and interests, this indicates that their design is not yet sound and mature and cannot gain the public's “trust.” This is also why virtual assets such as Bitcoin and stablecoins, although they attempted to become payment instruments at their inception, remain objects of speculation on trading platforms to this day and are rarely used in real economic transactions. On the other hand, analysis of the “practice” of money concerns its 3 basic functions: being universally used as a medium of exchange, a store of value, and a unit of pricing (accounting). These 3 basic functions are also all founded on the public's trust in central bank money.

Advancing central bank digital currency (CBDC) is not just about digitizing cash. It also involves payment system innovation and affects financial stability and the implementation of monetary policy. Therefore, major countries' research into CBDC is not based on a race to issue it first, but rather on ensuring that CBDC issuance meets
