Summary
I. International economic and financial conditions
(1) Global economic growth is expected to slow this year, and inflation is expected to decline
1. Most central banks have simultaneously adopted tight monetary policies to combat high inflation. Since October of last year (2022), global economic growth has slowed. Since the beginning of this year (2023), the global economic outlook has become slightly more optimistic. S&P Global Markit Intelligence (hereinafter referred to as S&P Global) has raised its forecast for global economic growth this year from 1.4% in October of last year to 2.0%, which is nevertheless still below last year’s 3.0%.
2. With prices of commodities such as crude oil falling, S&P Global forecasts that global inflation will fall from 7.6% last year to 5.3% this year.
(2) Most economies, including the United States and Europe, maintain their pace of interest rate increases, while Japan and China continue monetary easing
1. In recent months, economies including the United States and Europe have maintained their pace of interest rate increases. However, as inflation has declined, the Federal Reserve System (Fed) of the United States has reduced the size of its interest rate increases. The European Central Bank (ECB) and the Bank of England (BoE) have only slightly reduced the size of their interest rate increases, as inflationary pressures remain high.
2. Although the Bank of Japan (BoJ) has widened the fluctuation range for 10-year government bond yields, it has maintained an accommodative monetary policy; the People’s Bank of China has also eased monetary policy to stimulate economic recovery.
(3) In recent months, the US Fed has slowed the pace of interest rate increases, and the economic performance of the United States and the euro area has been better than expected. Global stock markets rebounded sharply, then softened as the Fed might again tighten monetary policy aggressively. The US dollar index first declined and then rebounded. Recently, 10-year government bond yields in the United States, Germany and Japan have fluctuated upward.
(4) The global economy faces multiple downside risks: (1) Tight labor markets in the United States and Europe and the reshoring of supply chains could cause inflation to rise again; (2) If US and European central banks tighten further because inflation does not cool as

II. Domestic economic and financial conditions
(1) The economy will grow moderately this year
1. In the 4th quarter of last year, private consumption recovered; however, global final demand declined and businesses continued to reduce inventories. Taiwan’s exports turned to negative growth of 8.65%, which also affected willingness to undertake private investment. The economic growth rate fell to -0.41%, and the full-year economic growth rate also fell to 2.45%, the lowest since 2017.
2. Last year, Taiwan’s real GDP grew by 12.84% compared with 2019, significantly outperforming major economies such as South Korea (6.08%), the United States (5.12%), the euro area (2.09%), Japan (-1.08%) and the United Kingdom (-0.39%).
3. The momentum of global economic and trade growth is expected to slow this year, which will restrain Taiwan’s export growth. Businesses also have a cautious view of future economic conditions, and private investment momentum will slow. However, domestic epidemic prevention measures and border controls have been relaxed, and people’s willingness to consume has increased. The economy is expected to grow moderately this year. Major domestic and foreign institutions’ forecasts for Taiwan’s economic growth rate this year average 2.21%.
(2) The unemployment rate continues to decline, and real wages grow slightly
1. With the domestic epidemic under control, commercial activity has gradually recovered, and the unemployment rate has continued to decline, reaching 3.50% in January of this year, the lowest since February 2001.
2. Last year, regular earnings of all employees increased by an average of 2.80% year on year, the highest since 2000, and total earnings grew by an average of 3.45%, the highest since 2019. Excluding price factors, real regular earnings declined by 0.15% year on year, while real total earnings grew only slightly, by 0.48%.

(3) The year-on-year CPI growth rate is expected to fall to about 2% this year
1. Last year, the year-on-year CPI growth rate was 2.95%, the highest since 2009, mainly affected by supply-side factors such as rising costs of imported raw materials, including crude oil and food. The year-on-year core CPI growth rate was 2.61%, also the highest since 2009.
2. Last year, Taiwan’s CPI rose by 4.73% compared with 2019, still far below major economies such as the United States (14.49%), the United Kingdom (12.83%), the euro area (11.46%) and South Korea (7.87%).
3. Taiwan’s price increases have been relatively moderate, mainly because Taiwan has actively adopted supply-side measures to respond to fluctuations in energy and food prices and reduce their spillover effects, which has greatly helped overall price stability. In addition, the Bank’s gradual tightening of monetary policy has helped restrain domestic inflation expectations.
4. In January of this year, the year-on-year CPI and core CPI growth rates rose to 3.04% and 2.98%, respectively, mainly because the Lunar New Year fell earlier this year than last year, bringing forward purchasing demand and the customary price increases for some service fees.
5. Looking ahead to this year, global supply-chain bottlenecks are easing and international freight rates are declining. In addition, prices of raw materials such as crude oil are expected to be lower than last year. Taiwan’s year-on-year CPI growth rate is expected to fall to about 2% this year; forecasts by major domestic and foreign institutions range from 1.40% to 2.60%, with an average of 1.94%.
(4) M2 year-on-year growth first rose and then fell, while long- and short-term interest rates increased
1. After rising to 7.37% in November of last year, M2 year-on-year growth fell back to 7.06% in December because of a decline in the growth of bank loans and investments, together with net outflows of foreign capital; the full-year average was 7.48%.

III. The Bank’s main operations
(1) The Bank has adopted moderate and gradual monetary tightening, helping restrain domestic inflation expectations and maintain price stability
1. Since last year, affected by supply-side factors such as rising costs of imported crude oil and food, Taiwan’s price increases have remained high. To restrain domestic inflation expectations and achieve the policy objectives of promoting price stability and assisting sound overall economic and financial development, the Bank has continued to adopt tight monetary policy, raising policy interest rates four times by a total of 0.625 percentage points, together with two increases in the New Taiwan dollar reserve requirement ratios totaling 0.5 percentage points.
2. Compared with economies such as the United States and Europe, Taiwan’s degree of monetary tightening has been relatively moderate. The main considerations are:
(1) Economies such as the United States and Europe have aggressively tightened monetary policy, international financial conditions have tightened, and the global economy has slowed markedly. As a small open economy, Taiwan is affected by spillovers through 3 channels: the real sector, prices and finance.
(2) Since last year, domestic price increases have mainly been affected by supply-side factors such as rising costs of imported crude oil and food; the government has continued to use supply-side measures to respond to fluctuations in energy and food prices, which has greatly helped overall price stability.
(3) Although domestic inflation shocks mainly stem from supply-side cost pressures, it is still necessary for the Bank to adopt appropriately tight monetary policy to constrain inflation expectations and maintain price stability. The Bank has raised policy interest rates and reserve requirement ratios, using both price and quantity measures, which helps restrain domestic inflation expectations.

(2) Since the implementation of the Bank’s selective credit control measures, banks’ real estate credit risks have been well controlled; the Bank maintains the current selective credit control measures to promote domestic financial stability. (3) The Bank regulates the foreign exchange market to maintain its smooth operation
1. The New Taiwan dollar depreciated against the US dollar last year and has stabilized since the beginning of this year
(1) Last year, foreign investors were net sellers of Taiwan stocks and remitted large amounts of funds out. At the end of last year, the New Taiwan dollar had depreciated by 9.83% against the US dollar compared with the end of 2021; since the beginning of this year, as foreign investors have been net buyers of Taiwan stocks, the New Taiwan dollar has appreciated by about 0.67% against the US dollar to date (2023/2/22).
(2) Last year, foreign investors’ cumulative net sales of Taiwan stocks totaled 1.14 trillion New Taiwan dollars (equivalent to about 391 hundred million US dollars), which were remitted out along with some dividends received, resulting in excess demand for US dollars in the domestic foreign exchange market. In the first and second halves of last year, the Bank’s net foreign exchange sales were 82.5 hundred million US dollars and 47.5 hundred million US dollars, respectively, totaling 130 hundred million US dollars.
(3) Taiwan is a small open economy with high trade dependence, and its foreign exchange market is shallow, making it necessary to maintain relative stability in the New Taiwan dollar exchange rate. Over the long term, the volatility of the New Taiwan dollar exchange rate against the US dollar has been lower than that of major currencies such as the Singapore dollar, euro, Japanese yen and Korean won. This benefits businesses’ pricing and operations and also helps domestic financial stability and economic growth.
2. The Bank has ample foreign currency liquidity
(1) At the end of January of this year, the Bank’s foreign exchange reserves were 5,571 hundred million US dollars, an increase of 22 hundred million US dollars compared with the end of last year, mainly reflecting returns from the investment and management of the Bank’s foreign exchange reserves and the appreciation of major currencies against the US dollar, which increased their value when expressed in US dollars.

Chair, Committee Members, Ladies and Gentlemen:
I am deeply honored to be invited to your Committee today to report on the Bank’s operations.

III. Recently, government bond yields in major economies have mostly risen, the US dollar has rebounded, and stock markets have softened
(1) Recently, 10-year government bond yields in the US, Germany and Japan have risen
Since October of last year, inflation in the US and the euro area moderated, and markets expected the Fed and ECB to slow the pace of rate increases, causing US and German 10-year government bond yields to fall. Subsequently, as the decline in US inflation fell short of expectations, inflationary pressure in the euro area remained high, and US and European labor markets remained tight, expectations of aggressive monetary tightening by the Fed and ECB rose again, causing US and German 10-year government bond yields to fluctuate upward. In mainland China, with the easing of lockdown measures and the successive introduction of economic stimulus policies, the economy was expected to recover, and 10-year government bond yields gradually rose. Recently, they fell slightly as the People's Bank of China injected large amounts of funds to increase market liquidity. In Japan, as the BoJ widened the fluctuation band for yield control, 10-year government bond yields rose to fluctuate at the upper limit of the band of 0.5%.

IV. The global economic outlook faces many downside risks
Global economic growth is expected to continue slowing this year, and the outlook faces many downside risks. Close attention should be paid to subsequent developments in the relevant circumstances.

Tight labor markets in the US and Europe and the reshoring of supply chains could cause inflation to rise again

If US and European central banks tighten further because inflation does not moderate as expected, the slowdown in economic growth could become greater

• Continued tightness in US and European labor markets could trigger a wage-price spiral. In addition, mainland China's post-pandemic recovery and the continuing Russia-Ukraine war could also push up commodity prices, all of which could again induce a rise in global inflation.

• The effects of aggressive interest rate increases by US, European and other central banks last year have continued to emerge. If inflation rises again, major central banks will inevitably tighten monetary policy further, and spillover effects through trade, finance and exchange-rate channels will intensify further, potentially increasing the extent of the global economic slowdown.

• In recent years, countries have strengthened the resilience of their own supply chains. As supply chains return to high-income countries, production costs may increase, which is also unfavorable to the moderation of global inflation.

• The US is expected to maintain high interest rates for a period of time, which could cause economies with large US dollar-denominated external debts to fall into debt distress. If this leads to substantial capital outflows, it could trigger turmoil in international financial markets.
•

Climate change increases uncertainty in commodity supplies, while geopolitical risks are also rising

Downside risks to mainland China's economy remain and could affect the global economic recovery
• The lack of reform in mainland China's real estate market and the continuing risk of default, high fiscal risks for local governments, and US restrictions on semiconductors constitute downside risks to its economy. They could constrain its economic performance and, in turn, affect the global economic recovery.

• Climate change has caused frequent natural disasters around the world, increasing uncertainty in supplies of energy, food and other items. Europe may also still face the risk of energy shortages in future winters. These all affect global economic and inflation conditions.
• The Russia-Ukraine war has intensified the global geopolitical trend toward confrontation between blocs. In addition to the continuing US-China rivalry, risks of conflict in North and South Korea, India and Pakistan, the Middle East and other regions are also rising, affecting global economic development and social stability.

(III) Compared with economies such as the United States and Europe, Taiwan's degree of monetary policy tightening is relatively moderate. The main considerations are as follows:
1. Economies such as the United States and Europe have actively tightened monetary policy, international financial conditions have tightened, and global economic activity has slowed markedly. Taiwan, as a small open economy, is affected by spillovers through 3 channels:
(1) Real-sector channel: Cooling global final demand, together with inventory adjustments by firms, has caused Taiwan's exports to register negative growth for several consecutive months since September of last year. This has also affected willingness to undertake private investment, constraining domestic economic growth; however, it also helps reduce domestic inflationary pressures.
(2) Price channel: Since the second half of last year, prices of raw materials such as crude oil have fallen as global demand declined. Together with continued improvement in global supply chain bottlenecks and a sharp drop in international freight rates, this will continue to ease domestic imported inflationary pressures. The annual growth rate of import prices denominated in US dollars has declined for several consecutive months; the annual growth rate of import prices denominated in New Taiwan dollars has also trended downward, declining for several consecutive months.
(3) Financial channel: Affected by the Fed's continued interest rate hikes and pessimism about the global economic outlook, foreign investors recorded net outward remittances last year, and Taiwan's stock market fluctuated downward along with international stock markets.
2. The rise in domestic prices since last year has mainly been affected by supply-side factors, such as increases in the costs of imported crude oil and food. The government's continued use of supply-side measures to respond to fluctuations in energy and food prices has been of considerable help in stabilizing overall prices.
3. Although the domestic inflation shock has mainly come from supply-side cost pressures, it is still necessary for the Bank to adopt moderately tight monetary policy to restrain inflation expectations and maintain price stability. The Bank's increases in policy interest rates and reserve requirement ratios, using both price and quantity measures, help curb domestic inflation expectations.
4. Since last year, price increases in Taiwan have been relatively low compared with those in the United States, Europe, the United Kingdom, and parts of the Asia-Pacific region,
19

and the degree of monetary policy tightening has also been more moderate.

IV. Management of the National Treasury
1. National treasury receipts and payments
(1) Collection of national treasury revenue
Last year, national treasury revenue collected by all treasury agent institutions totaled 4 trillion 6,368 hundred million yuan, an increase of 4,389 hundred million yuan or 10.46% from 2021. In January of this year, 3,573 hundred million yuan was collected, a decrease of 1,304 hundred million yuan or 26.74% from the same period of last year.
(2) Disbursement of national treasury expenditures
Last year, national treasury expenditures disbursed by all treasury agent institutions totaled 4 trillion 5,604 hundred million yuan, an increase of 3,769 hundred million yuan or 9.01% from 2021. In January of this year, 4,359 hundred million yuan was disbursed, a decrease of 858 hundred million yuan or 16.45% from the same period of last year.
(3) National treasury deposit balance
At the end of last year, the national treasury deposit balance under management was 1,441 hundred million yuan, an increase of 764 hundred million yuan or 112.85% from the end of 2021. At the end of January of this year, the balance was 655 hundred million yuan, an increase of 318 hundred million yuan or 94.36% from the same period of last year.
