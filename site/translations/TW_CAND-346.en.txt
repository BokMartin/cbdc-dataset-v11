VII. Explanation of the Results of the Bank's Outsourced Questionnaire Survey on the Topic of Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC)

    The operation of the modern monetary system is based on central bank money, building a diverse, convenient, safe and trusted payment system; in recent years, CBDC has become a focus of research internationally. If research and testing establish its feasibility, it could not only serve as a new foundation for future digital payments, but the central bank will also continue to provide the foundation of trust in the monetary system.

    To respond to developments in the digital age, the Bank began researching CBDC1 (i.e., the digital New Taiwan dollar) in 2019 and has completed two stages of research and testing2. It is currently at the prototype-building stage3, in step with advanced countries worldwide; three workstreams4 are currently underway. Among these, for the work on broad communication, an outsourced questionnaire survey was undertaken from April of last year (2023) and was successfully completed in April of this year (2024). This report aims to explain to the public the key survey results and subsequent related plans; it also reiterates that, if the Bank decides to issue CBDC in the future, taking the digital divide into consideration, it will still retain cash issuance to take account of the preferences and habits of particular groups.

(I) Origins of international research on CBDC

1. Developments in the digital age may reshape the forms of payment services

    The operation of the modern monetary system is based on central bank money, supporting the development of commercial bank money and other forms of money5, and together building a sound payment system centered on the central bank and financial institutions. However, the current payment market is no longer limited to services provided by traditional financial institutions. The entry of non-bank payment institutions, technological innovation and other factors may all reshape the forms of payment services.

2. Regardless of how financial technology develops, the role played by the central bank and the foundation of trust it provides for the monetary system will remain key to a sound financial system

(1) Regardless of future technological innovations, central bank money remains the anchor6 of the monetary system, ensuring the exchange of commercial bank money for central bank money at 1：1

(II) Latest global development trends in CBDC

1. Apart from a small number of countries that have formally issued retail CBDC, other major advanced countries are still at the proof-of-concept or prototype-building stage, and currently have no specific issuance timetable.

2. Most countries that have currently issued CBDC are countries with less adequate financial infrastructure, such as the Bahamas and Nigeria, which hope thereby to promote financial inclusion. However, developments since issuance have fallen short of expectations9; a small number of countries have entered the pilot stage, such as mainland China and Thailand; major advanced countries such as Japan10, the euro area11 and the United Kingdom12 are still at the proof-of-concept or prototype-building stage.

(2) To avoid members of the public converting large amounts of deposits into CBDC, if a decision is made to issue it in the future, it will initially be issued without interest to maintain the existing monetary policy transmission mechanism; however, the interest-bearing function will be retained to preserve future policy flexibility13.

3. Other important designs and functions

(1) Wallet tiering [錢包分級]: Taking risk management into consideration, CBDC wallets will be divided into unregistered [不記名] and registered [記名] wallets14 according to the type of holder, method of opening and degree of verification of identity [身分驗證]. According to risk levels, the CBDC platform sets upper limits on wallet stored-value amounts [錢包儲值金額及交易金額上限] and transaction limits; intermediaries may also set their own limits based on their own risk considerations, but these must not exceed the limits set by the platform.

(2) Privacy [隱私] and personal data [個人資料] protection:
      ‒ Users opening unregistered wallets only need to provide a mobile phone number and do not need to provide other personal data, thereby also taking account of the public's privacy needs.
      ‒ When users open registered wallets, intermediaries carry out customer due diligence (KYC) [認識你的客戶(KYC)] procedures and must properly safeguard and manage users' personal data. Intermediaries may only access [查閱] relevant data pertaining to themselves and their own users. The Bank may only access user transaction data processed using de-identification [去識別化] technology.

(3) Platform functions: In addition to supporting basic functions such as transfers between wallets, purchases, conversion (referring to mutual conversion between cash, bank deposits and CBDC), and automatic conversion/top-up15, it can also support value-added functions such as digital vouchers and authorized debits16.

(4) Information security management:
      ‒ Overall platform security: A defense in depth (DiD)17 architecture is adopted, with the Bank, intermediaries and users jointly achieving security management objectives. In this, the central bank maintains platform security; intermediaries establish information security management systems to ensure uninterrupted operations; users comply with intermediaries' rules for secure use of wallet

4. Using the preliminary CBDC framework and design planned above as basic material for broad communication, and continuing the two workstreams of refining platform design and studying the legal framework

(1) Broad communication to understand the public's awareness of and needs regarding CBDC, and to obtain support:
     ‒ Mainly divided into two major phases:
         First phase (from April last year to April this year), outsourced questionnaire survey to solicit opinions broadly: The Bank commissioned the Taiwan Institute of Economic Research to conduct a quantitative questionnaire survey of the general public, industry, government institutions and academia on topics such as CBDC awareness, needs, design and privacy. It was completed in April this year.
         Second phase (expected to start in January 2025), specific and in-depth communication: With reference to the survey results, review and revise the preliminary CBDC framework and design. In the future, based on the revised content, several public hearings, briefing sessions or forums will be held to conduct broader and more specific exchanges with stakeholders, and thereby strengthen CBDC outreach and raise public awareness.

(2) Refining platform design and adopting more robust and mature technologies:
     ‒ The CBDC platform already has digital currency lifecycle (issuance, circulation, cancellation) management and various basic functions. Work is currently continuing on developing ideas for various innovative applications and value-added functions, including conditional payments such as digital vouchers and authorized debits; in addition, taking into account the digital divide and the payment needs of disadvantaged groups or groups in remote areas, research and testing of offline payment [離線支付] technology are also being conducted; and, in response to the trend toward tokenization, the feasibility of using wholesale CBDC to settle tokenized assets is being tested.

(3) Studying the legal framework: In conjunction with the above two workstreams, the legal framework of our country is being studied in parallel, and an inventory is being made of laws and regulations that may need to be established or amended in connection with issuing CBDC, in order to clearly delineate the rights and obligations of all participants in the CBDC ecosystem and strengthen market trust in CBDC.

     －Legal scholars:
         If CBDC is issued using a two-tier architecture [雙層式架構], then, regarding the reciprocal rights and obligations between intermediaries and the public33, and between the central bank and the public34 in issuance, circulation or safekeeping: more than 6 in 10 scholars believe that a contractual relationship should exist between intermediaries and the public, such as fixed-term contracts, contracts of deposit for consumption, a good administrator, protection of the right to privacy [隱私權], and agreements on compensation for losses (Figure 22); nearly 6 in 10 scholars believe that a legal relationship should exist between the central bank and the public, such as financial supervision obligations, obligations to guarantee payment35, protection of the right to privacy, and ensuring that, after digitalization, the risk of inability to pay due to factors such as electricity cannot be transferred to consumers or intermediaries to bear (Figure 23).          CBDC legal framework: If CBDC is issued using a two-tier architecture, nearly 7 in 10 scholars believe that the “Central Bank of the Republic of China (Taiwan) Act” should first be amended before CBDC can be issued. (V) Conclusion
1. The Bank continues to refine technology and improve CBDC design and functions to meet the needs of future digital economic development and the public
(1) The results of this questionnaire survey show that many members of the public have a need for offline payments, especially card-based wallets [卡式錢包]. Taking into account technical difficulty, risks, etc., the Bank will continue to conduct relevant research and experiments and monitor the evolution of mainstream technologies internationally, so as to make offline payments resilient and widely accessible; if a decision is made in the future to issue CBDC, implementation in stages may be considered, and the most suitable timing for introduction will then be assessed in light of the maturity of technologies internationally and the public’s actual needs and necessity. In addition, as remote areas or disadvantaged groups have a higher proportion of cash payments owing to preferences or habits, the issuance of cash will still be retained when introducing CBDC, so as to also take their needs into account.
(2) Regarding some members of the public’s concerns about privacy and the use of personal data [個人資料], the Bank will continue to study and deliberate on a rigorous data management system, a clearly defined scope of data use47, comprehensive privacy protection mechanisms, and the strengthening of information security technologies, to enhance public trust and reduce concerns.
(3) In response to developments in the digital era, how to apply the technological advantages of tokenization [代幣化] and programmability [可程式化], using central bank money as the final settlement instrument, has recently become a focus of attention for central banks in various countries; to explore possible developments and potential applications, the Bank has also planned experiments related to settling tokenized assets using wholesale CBDC48.
2. The Bank will undertake the second phase of broad communication work
(1) Based on the results of this outsourced questionnaire survey, the preliminary CBDC framework and design will be reviewed and revised. In the future, several public hearings, explanatory meetings, or forums will be held on the basis of the revised content, to conduct more concrete, broad, and in-depth exchanges and communication with stakeholders, and thereby strengthen outreach on CBDC and raise public awareness.
(2) As for the wide scope of CBDC-related legal-system issues, in addition to using the above-mentioned occasions to exchange views with various sectors, the Bank will also, in line with the progress of its CBDC-related work, continue to collect information on the status and developments of legislative amendments concerning CBDC in major countries internationally, and study and deliberate on our country’s legal framework.
(3) After the above work is completed, whether to implement a pilot plan will then be studied, deliberated on, and assessed49.
