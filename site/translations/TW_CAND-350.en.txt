Summary
I. International economic and financial conditions
(I) Global economic growth this year is expected to be lower than last year; inflationary pressures are rising
1. Since the end of September last (2025) year, driven by the strong development of emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence (artificial intelligence, AI), global manufacturing activity has gradually returned to an expansionary path. However, in mid-February this (2026) year, the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that reciprocal tariffs were invalid, and the U.S. government rapidly introduced alternative tariff policies, increasing trade policy uncertainty; at the end of February, the United States and Israel jointly launched air strikes on Iran, shipping through the Strait of Hormuz was interrupted, and international energy prices soared, increasing downside risks to the global economy. In March this year, S&P Global Market Intelligence (hereinafter referred to as S&P Global) forecast that global economic growth this year would decline from 2.9% last year to 2.6%, a downward revision of 0.3 percentage points from its February forecast; most forecasts for major economies were also revised downward.
2. The recent hostilities in the Middle East have caused a sharp rise in international prices of crude oil and other energy, increasing inflationary pressures. In March this year, S&P Global forecast global inflation of 3.2% this year, unchanged from last year, but revised upward by 0.4 percentage points from its February forecast; most forecasts for major economies were also revised upward.
(II) Since October last year, the Fed has cut interest rates twice, the ECB has kept policy rates unchanged, the BoJ has raised interest rates, and the People's Bank of China has continued its accommodative monetary policy
1. Since early October last year, in view of rising downside risks in the labor market, the U.S. Federal Reserve System (Fed) lowered its policy rate by 0.25 percentage points each in October and December; it kept interest rates unchanged in January and March this year. The European Central Bank (ECB) kept policy rates unchanged, in view of euro area inflation having reached the 2% target and the economy remaining resilient.
2. In view of inflation continuing to exceed 2% and the expectation that momentum in corporate wage increases would

In view of insufficient strength in economic growth and the recovery of inflation, the People's Bank of China continued its accommodative monetary policy, lowering the interest rates on relending, rediscounting, and various structural monetary policy instruments by 0.25 percentage points each in January this year.
(III) Recent hostilities in the Middle East have increased inflation expectations, and government bond yields in most major economies have risen; safe-haven funds have flowed into the U.S. dollar, and the U.S. dollar index has rebounded; stock market volatility has intensified, and the global stock price index has fallen back from its historical high.
(IV) The global economy faces shocks from numerous uncertainties: (1) rising geopolitical risks; (2) developments in U.S. trade policy and their effects; (3) the path of monetary policy adjustments by major central banks; (4) the economic benefits of AI development and its effects; (5) weak domestic demand and overcapacity in mainland China.
II. Domestic economic and financial conditions
(I) Taiwan's economy is expected to grow steadily this year
1. In the 4th quarter of last year, growth in Taiwan's exports and private consumption exceeded expectations, and economic growth reached 12.65%, the highest single-quarter rate in 38 years. Full-year economic growth was 8.68%, the highest in nearly 15 years.
2. Business opportunities in applications of emerging AI technologies are expected to continue expanding this year, which will continue to drive steady growth in Taiwan's exports and contribute to momentum in private investment; growth in private consumption is also expected to strengthen. Taking into account the effects of U.S. tariff policies and hostilities in the Middle East, the Bank forecasts Taiwan's economic growth this year at 7.28%. The average forecast by major domestic and foreign institutions for Taiwan's economic growth this year is 6.40%.
3. This year's pattern of economic growth is similar to last year's, with “net foreign demand” and “capital formation” contributing to Taiwan's economic growth. Their combined contribution to economic growth is expected to reach 5.55 percentage points, accounting for 76.2% of growth.

4. The Bank currently assesses that the effects of the new U.S. tariff policies on Taiwan's economic growth this year should be limited; hostilities in the Middle East add to downside risks to the global economy, and the extent of their effects on Taiwan's economy depends on their duration, intensity, and geographical scope.
(II) The unemployment rate has trended downward, the number of enterprises and people implementing reduced working hours and leave has declined, and wages have grown steadily
1. Since October last year, the unemployment rate has declined month by month, reaching 3.29% in January this year; it rose slightly to 3.32% in February, mainly affected by the wave of job changes around the Lunar New Year, but was still the lowest for the same month since 2001.
2. As of March 15 this year, 240 enterprises and 3,839 people were implementing reduced working hours and leave, decreases of 158 enterprises and 4,666 people compared with the end of September last year. Of these, manufacturing had the largest decrease, of 4,595 people.

Last year, nominal regular earnings of employees in industry and services increased by 3.09% year on year, the largest increase since 2000; total earnings increased by 3.91% year on year, the second highest in nearly 15 years. After removing price effects, real regular earnings increased by 1.40% year on year last year, the highest in nearly 5 years; real total earnings increased by 2.21% year on year, the highest in nearly 7 years.
(III) Inflation gradually declined last year, and the inflation outlook for this year is moderate; the Bank will continue to closely monitor the effects of hostilities in the Middle East on Taiwan's inflation
1. Last year, the annual CPI growth rate continued to decline from 2.18% in 2024 to 1.66%, the lowest since 2021. The annual growth rate of core CPI, excluding fruit, vegetables, and energy, also continued to decline from 1.88% in 2024 to 1.66%. The average annual growth rates of CPI and core CPI in January and February this year were 1.23% and 1.93%, respectively.
2. The recent outbreak of hostilities in the Middle East has pushed up international prices of crude oil and other energy. Taking into account the supply-side measures of the government's energy price stabilization mechanisms, the Bank has revised upward its forecasts for Taiwan's annual CPI and core CPI growth rates this year to 1.80% and 1.75%. In addition, funding demand increased around the Lunar New Year in February, and the annual growth rate of banks' working-capital loans to private enterprises, public enterprises, and the government rose. The annual growth rate of bank loans and investments rose month by month, reaching 7.37% at the end of February.

3. Since September last year, with the Bank's open market operations, market funding conditions have remained relatively stable, and bank and money market interest rates have fluctuated slightly.

III. The Bank's main operations
(I) The Bank kept policy rates unchanged in March this year, helping the sound development of the overall economy and financial system
In March this year, the Bank's Board of Directors kept policy rates unchanged, with the following main considerations:
(1) The domestic inflation outlook for this year remains moderate; (2) the domestic economy is expected to grow steadily; (3) to respond prudently to uncertainty in the global economic and financial outlook and to the possible effects of hostilities in the Middle East and U.S. economic and trade policies on domestic prices and the economy.
(II) The Bank moderately adjusted its selective credit control measures, raising the maximum loan-to-value ratio for natural persons' second home purchase loans nationwide from 5 tenths to 6 tenths, while maintaining the absence of a grace period. The main considerations were:
1. Following the Bank's seventh adjustment of selective credit control measures in September 2024, together with banks' implementation of self-management of their total real estate lending, results have gradually become apparent:
(1) As growth in real estate lending by all banks slowed, the concentration of real estate loans declined, from a peak of 37.6% at the end of June 2024 to 36.0% at the end of February this year.
(2) Bank credit resources are being provided on a priority basis for home purchase loans to those without owner-occupied housing, and for funding uses that support government policies, such as urban renewal and reconstruction of unsafe and old buildings.
(3) Speculative activity in the housing market has decreased, and transaction volume has contracted markedly; public expectations of rising house prices have declined, the existing-home price index has gradually fallen from its peak, and price increases for newly launched projects have also slowed.
2. The release of a large quantity of newly built housing in recent years may continue to increase the pressure from newly completed homes awaiting sale; a moderate adjustment of the Bank's credit control measures will help reduce the stock of newly completed homes awaiting sale.
3. The main reasons for this moderate adjustment to the loan-to-value ratio for natural persons' second home purchase loans nationwide:
(1) In the 4th quarter of last year, the number of borrowers and the amount of second home purchase loans handled by domestic banks declined by 37.3% and 40.0% year on year, respectively, followed by further year-on-year declines of 10.7% and 10.8% in January and February this year.

(2) Responding to public petitions that applications for second home loans serve the needs of family members or the applicants themselves to purchase homes for their own residence, consistent with the Bank's principle of guiding credit resources to give priority to owner-occupancy needs.
4. Although transactions in the domestic housing market have currently cooled, growth in bank real estate lending has slowed, and the concentration of real estate loans has gradually declined, it is not yet appropriate to substantially relax credit controls. The main reasons are: (1) although domestic house prices have reversed course, the adjustment has been limited, and the home loan burden on the public remains heavy. A substantial relaxation of credit controls may cause public expectations of rising house prices to re-emerge, which would undo the results of the Bank's seven rounds of credit controls; (2) the wealth effect from the sharp rise in Taiwan's stock market over the past 1 year, and the recent rise in oil prices driven by hostilities in the Middle East, may increase inflation expectations; both may cause funds to move into the housing market.
5. In the future, banks must still submit real estate lending-related data to the Bank each month, and the Bank will strengthen targeted financial examinations to review each bank's compliance with the Bank's regulations and its internal controls over total real estate lending; at the same time, it will closely monitor the possible effects of real estate-related policies on the housing market, review the effectiveness of credit control measures on a rolling basis, and adjust the content of relevant measures in a timely manner, to promote financial stability and sound banking operations.
(III) The Bank intervenes in the foreign exchange market to maintain its smooth operation
1. Last year, the supply of U.S. dollars in the domestic foreign exchange market exceeded demand, and the Bank's net foreign exchange purchases were 76.9 hundred million U.S. dollars.
(1) In the first half of last year, the U.S. dollar index (DXY) fell by 10.70%, the supply of U.S. dollars in the domestic foreign exchange market exceeded demand, and the Bank's net foreign exchange purchases were 132.5 hundred million U.S. dollars; the New Taiwan dollar appreciated by 9.63% against the U.S. dollar. In the second half of the year, the U.S. dollar index appreciated by 1.49%. During the same period, foreign investors took profits in Taiwan stocks and remitted funds outward along with some dividends received, resulting in excess demand for U.S. dollars in the domestic foreign exchange market. The Bank intervened with net foreign exchange sales of 55.6 hundred million U.S. dollars, and the New Taiwan dollar depreciated by 4.89% against the U.S. dollar.
(2) For the whole of last year, the Bank's cumulative net foreign exchange purchases were 76.9 hundred million U.S. dollars (equivalent to approximately 0.8% of GDP). The New Taiwan dollar appreciated by 4.27% against the U.S. dollar, less than major currencies such as the Swiss franc (14.48%), the euro

(IV) Progress in promoting the redesign of New Taiwan dollar banknotes and the implementation of public participation
1. On October 23 last year, the Bank formally launched the New Taiwan dollar banknote redesign project and established an interdisciplinary “Advisory Committee on Themes for the Redesign of New Taiwan Dollar Banknotes”; following discussion by the committee, “The Beauty of Taiwan” was established as the theme of the new banknote series, and 12 denomination themes were selected as options for soliciting public opinion.
2. To expand social participation and enhance public acceptance, following the committee's recommendation, the Bank held a vote on denomination themes for “The Beauty of Taiwan” banknote series on its official website, attracting more than 19 ten thousand participations; the voting results will be incorporated into professional judgment and overall considerations as an important reference for the design of the new banknotes.
3. Regarding the denomination configuration, with reference to banknote issuance practices in major countries (regions), the current configuration of 5 New Taiwan dollar banknote denominations should be appropriate, and most countries adopt the practice of redesigning all denominations; also considering that some members of the public still have demand for banknotes with denominations beginning with 2, and that their issuance has cost

(V) Improving the payment and settlement system and providing final settlement services for New Taiwan dollar interbank fund transfers
Last year, the transaction value processed by the Interbank Funds Transfer System reached 601 trillion dollars, an increase of 3.2% from 2024; regarding the retail payment system, the transaction value processed by the Interbank Financial Information System (abbreviated as the FISC System), operated by Financial Information Service Co., Ltd., reached 247 trillion dollars, an increase of 2.8% from 2024.

II. International Economic and Financial Conditions
1. Global economic growth is slowing, and inflationary pressures are rising
(I) Global economic growth is expected to decline this (2026) year
Since the end of September last (2025) year, driven by the strong development of emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence (artificial intelligence, AI), global manufacturing activity has gradually returned to an expansionary path. In mid-February this year, the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that the government's reciprocal tariffs invoking the “International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA)” exceeded statutory authorization. Subsequently, the U.S. government announced that it would instead impose tariffs globally under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974, and adopt other measures such as Section 301 to reinforce its tariff policies, increasing trade policy uncertainty; at the end of February, the United States and Israel jointly launched air strikes on Iran, after which shipping through the Strait of Hormuz was interrupted and international energy prices soared, increasing downside risks to the global economy. In March this year, S&P Global Market Intelligence (hereinafter referred to as S&P Global), assuming that the war would ease before mid-April this year, forecast that global economic growth this year would decline from 2.9% last year to 2.6%, a downward revision of 0.3 percentage points from its pre-war forecast (mid-February this year); most forecasts for major economies were also revised downward.

(II) The U.S. dollar index fluctuated, and most major economies’ currencies depreciated against the U.S. dollar

Since the end of September last year, global risk asset prices have fluctuated sharply, and funds flowed into the U.S. dollar as a safe haven, at one point driving up the U.S. Dollar Index (DXY)1. After January this year, the United States used the Greenland issue to put pressure on its European allies, and President Trump stated that he was not worried about U.S. dollar depreciation, resulting in a relatively large decline in the U.S. dollar index; subsequently, safe-haven funds flowed into the U.S. dollar because of the Middle East war, and the U.S. dollar index gradually recovered.

Comparing March 25 this year with the end of September last year, major economies’ currencies showed mixed appreciation and depreciation against the U.S. dollar. Among them, Malaysia saw capital inflows as AI infrastructure drove foreign direct investment, and the Malaysian ringgit appreciated by 6.1% against the U.S. dollar, a relatively large increase; the Japanese yen depreciated by 7.2% against the U.S. dollar, a relatively large decline, because the market expected the Takaichi government to continue the accommodative monetary policy of Abenomics, compounded by expectations that rising energy prices would push up import costs and affect economic growth.

IV. The Global Economic Outlook Faces Numerous Uncertainties

The global economic outlook this year is expected to face numerous uncertainties, including heightened geopolitical risks, developments in U.S. trade policy and their effects, the paths of monetary policy adjustment by major central banks, the economic benefits of AI development and their effects, and weak domestic demand and overcapacity in mainland China. Continued attention to subsequent developments and effects is still needed.

Heightened geopolitical risks

Developments in U.S. trade policy and their effects

⚫ The Middle East war remains at a stalemate, and the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz has disrupted global energy supplies, sharply pushing up energy prices and exposing the world to stagflationary pressures. Its effects on the global and regional economies will depend on the duration, intensity and geographical scope of the tensions; meanwhile, continuing issues such as China–Japan relations and Russia–Ukraine negotiations also sustain global geopolitical uncertainty, which could suppress businesses’ willingness to invest and add variables to the global economic and inflation outlook.

⚫ The United States is currently imposing additional tariffs on all imports under Section 122 of the Trade Act, and is intensifying investigations under Section 301 of the Trade Act and Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act, reinforcing its tariff policy; however, the applicability of the relevant legal bases and the investigation results remain highly uncertain. In addition, other legal bases may still be invoked to impose additional tariffs in the future, and bilateral trade may even require renegotiation, making policy continuity difficult to predict and increasing risks to business operations and supply chain adjustments.

Paths of monetary policy adjustment by major central banks
⚫ U.S. employment has weakened, and the situation in the Middle East could cause inflation to rise again, placing the Fed’s decision-making in a dilemma; inflation in the euro area remains stable and the economy is resilient, but uncertainty over external factors has increased sharply, and the ECB does not commit in advance to an interest rate path; in Japan, rising energy costs are unfavorable to the economy and prices, and the BoJ is closely monitoring developments in the Middle East and adjusting monetary policy cautiously; mainland China’s economy relies on policy support, and the People’s Bank of China will maintain monetary easing.
⚫ The direction of monetary policy at major central banks is bound to influence international capital flows and add to financial market volatility.

The economic benefits of AI development and their effects
⚫ The AI development boom has driven investment in related industries, but if future economic benefits fall short of expectations, the massive investment in AI could face a risk of becoming a bubble, in turn causing asset prices to fall. The negative wealth effect will weaken global demand and affect global financial stability.
⚫ On the other hand, if AI develops smoothly and is widely adopted, it will reshape business management, industry operations and modes of competition, potentially causing businesses to be eliminated from the market and companies to reduce their workforces, affecting business owners and workers, harming employment and, in turn, affecting economic stability.

Weak domestic demand and overcapacity in mainland China
⚫ Mainland China’s housing market remains depressed and consumer confidence is weak; although the government has increased the strength of fiscal expansion, its effectiveness in boosting the real economy remains to be verified.
⚫ Overcapacity is a serious problem, and involution-style low-price competition within industries compresses profit margins, which is unfavorable to economic structural transformation; if mainland China continues to export large quantities of surplus products, this could affect related industries in other countries, intensify global trade frictions and increase protectionism.

Source: The Bank’s compilation of views of major international institutions and media reports

(I) In the fourth quarter of last year, Taiwan’s export growth exceeded expectations, and private consumption momentum recovered, bringing the economic growth rate to 12.65%, the highest quarterly rate in 38 years; the full-year economic growth rate was 8.68%, the highest since 2011

In the fourth quarter of last year, strong demand for applications of emerging technologies such as AI and the continued expansion of capital expenditure by major international cloud service providers (Cloud Service Provider, CSP) drove substantial growth in domestic exports of electronic components and information, communications and audiovisual products. In addition, the government’s universal cash payment, commodity tax reduction and exemption measures, and the wealth effect of record highs in Taiwan’s stock market helped revive private consumption momentum. Taiwan’s export and private consumption growth exceeded expectations, bringing the economic growth rate to 12.65%, the highest quarterly rate in 38 years. III. Last year, the New Taiwan dollar appreciated against the US dollar, but its exchange rate was stable relative to major currencies
(1) Last year, the supply of US dollars in the domestic foreign exchange market exceeded demand, and the Bank's net foreign exchange purchases amounted to 76.9 hundred million US dollars
1. First half of the year: the market was concerned about rising uncertainty surrounding the US President's new policies, which weakened economic confidence among businesses and the public, and the US Dollar Index (DXY) fell by 10.70%. In April last year, the implementation of US reciprocal tariffs was suspended for 90 days, global stock markets recovered, foreign investors remitted funds to invest in Taiwan stocks, and domestic firms benefited from demand for advance shipments. In addition, media speculation and amplification of the possibility that the US government might require its trading partners' currencies to appreciate created strong expectations of New Taiwan dollar appreciation in the domestic foreign exchange market, and firms' supply of US dollars increased. During the same period, the Bank's net foreign exchange purchases amounted to 132.5 hundred million US dollars to maintain stability in the domestic foreign exchange market, and the New Taiwan dollar appreciated by 9.63% against the US dollar.
2. Second half of the year: in view of signs of cooling in the US labor market, and the Fed's indication that it would pause interest rate cuts after cutting rates by a total of 3 increments of a quarter percentage point from September to December, the US Dollar Index appreciated by 1.49%. During the same period, expectations of New Taiwan dollar appreciation faded, and foreign investors took profits on Taiwan stocks and remitted funds outward together with part of their dividend distributions. Excess demand for US dollars emerged in the domestic foreign exchange market, and the Bank intervened to adjust conditions, making net foreign exchange sales of 55.6 hundred million US dollars (the Bank's net foreign exchange purchases in the 3rd quarter were 12.87 hundred million US dollars, and its net foreign exchange sales in the 4th quarter were 68.48 hundred million US dollars). The New Taiwan dollar depreciated by 4.89% against the US dollar.
3. For the whole of last year, the Bank's cumulative net foreign exchange purchases amounted to 76.9 hundred million US dollars (equivalent to approximately 0.8% of GDP), and the New Taiwan dollar appreciated by 4.27% against the US dollar, less than major currencies such as the Swiss franc (14.48%), euro (13.44%), Australian dollar (7.84%), and Singapore dollar (6.25%).

IV. Progress in promoting the redesign of New Taiwan dollar banknotes and implementation of civic participation

(I) The Bank has formed and convened the “Advisory Committee on Themes for the Redesign of New Taiwan Dollar Banknotes (hereinafter referred to as the Advisory Committee),” and has reached several concrete conclusions and recommendations, which will serve as important references for the Bank's subsequent related planning

1. Establishing the Advisory Committee and conducting civic participation

Following the formal launch of the New Taiwan dollar banknote redesign project on October 23 of last year, the Bank has formed the Advisory Committee and held 3 meetings. In addition to representatives of the Bank, the committee includes 12 external experts and scholars covering professional fields such as graphic communications, animals and plants, ecology, architecture, technology, art, education and sampling statistics.

(1) Selecting “The Beauty of Taiwan” as the theme for the new banknote series

Banknotes symbolize the national image, and their graphic design should keep pace with changes in the times and social progress. The Bank originally proposed 3 themes for the new banknote series, including “The Beauty of Taiwan,” “Taiwan's Vitality” and “A Diverse and Inclusive Taiwan.” Following study and full discussion, the Advisory Committee recommended adopting “The Beauty of Taiwan” as the series theme.

(2) Selecting 12 denomination themes as options for soliciting public opinion

In line with “The Beauty of Taiwan” series theme, the Bank initially proposed 16 denomination themes. Following voting and discussion by the Advisory Committee, 12 denomination themes were ultimately put forward as options for soliciting public opinion: “Harmony and Inclusion,” “Sustainable Development,” “The Spirit of Sports,” “Art and Aesthetics,” “The Beauty of Architecture,” “Technological Innovation,” “Folk Festivals,” “The Beauty of Crafts,” “Island Ecology,” “The Beauty of Protected Plants,” “The Beauty of Protected Animals” and “The Brilliance of Women.”

(3) Completing online voting on denomination themes

To increase the public's sense of identification with the new banknotes and build social consensus, the Bank, in accordance with
