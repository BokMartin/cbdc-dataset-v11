I. International economic and financial conditions

(1) Global economic growth this year is expected to be slightly lower than last year and to slow further next year; the inflation rate will continue to decline

1. Since April of this year (2025), although the United States' reciprocal tariff rates have been preliminarily determined, reciprocal tariffs and fentanyl tariffs face disputes over their legality. In addition, the recent renewed escalation of the US–China trade war has affected markets, and many variables remain in the global economic outlook. S&P Global Market Intelligence (hereinafter referred to as S&P Global) expects the global economic growth rate to decline slightly from 2.8% last year (2024) to 2.7% this year, and to decline further to 2.6% next year (2026).

2. As uncertainty about the global economic outlook remains high, restraining demand growth, and international commodity prices, including crude oil, have declined, the global inflation rate is expected to continue to fall. S&P Global forecasts that the global inflation rate will decline from 4.5% last year to 3.3% this year, and decline further to 3.1% next year.

(2) Through the end of this year, the Fed is expected to continue cutting interest rates, the ECB to keep policy rates unchanged, the BoJ has prospects of raising interest rates, and the People's Bank of China to maintain its accommodative stance

1. In September of this year, the US Federal Reserve System (Fed) lowered its policy rate by 0.25 percentage points because downside risks to employment conditions had increased, leading markets to expect that the Fed would continue cutting interest rates. In addition, Fed Chair Powell stated in October that signs of a gradual tightening of liquidity were currently appearing, and that the Fed might stop reducing its balance sheet in the coming months. The European Central Bank (ECB) kept its policy rates unchanged in July and September of this year, and markets expect policy rates to remain unchanged through year-end.

2. Considering that uncertainty about the impact of tariffs on the economy and prices remained high, the Bank of Japan (BoJ) decided in May, June, July and September to keep its policy rate unchanged; however, if tariff uncertainty declines and the virtuous cycle of wage growth and inflation meets expectations, it will still maintain its stance of raising

II. Domestic economic and financial conditions

(1) Taiwan's economy is expected to grow steadily this year and moderately next year

1. In the first half of this year, demand for applications of emerging technologies such as AI was strong, and export and private investment growth exceeded expectations, with the economic growth rate reaching 6.75%. In the second half of the year, because of the higher base for private investment and subdued momentum in private consumption due to uncertainty over US tariff measures, the Bank forecasts that the economic growth rate will decline to 2.51%, with growth for the full year at 4.55%.

2. Since the Bank's Board of Directors meeting in September of this year, Taiwan's exports and private investment have performed better than expected. In addition, active trading in Taiwan stocks and successive new highs have added momentum to private consumption. In recent months, most major institutions have revised upward their forecasts for Taiwan's economic growth rate this year, with an average of 5.02%. The Bank will continue to examine the impact of the latest factors on Taiwan's economic growth this year and will publicly announce its latest forecast for economic growth this year after the Bank's Board of Directors meeting in December.

3. Looking ahead to next year, growth in Taiwan's exports and private investment is expected to slow, but private consumption

(2) Bank credit resources are provided on a priority basis for home purchase loans to people without owner-occupied housing and for funding uses aligned with government policies, such as urban renewal and reconstruction of unsafe and old buildings: At the end of August of this year, the ratio of the outstanding balance of home purchase loans made by domestic banks to people without owner-occupied housing to the outstanding balance of loans for house purchases continued to rise, from 57.0% at the end of January 2023 to 63.1%. The ratio of loans for urban renewal and reconstruction of unsafe and old buildings to construction loans of all banks also rose from 19.5% at the end of September of last year to 24.0%.

(3) Both growth in real estate lending and the concentration of real estate lending have declined: Annual growth rates of the outstanding balances of real estate loans, loans for house purchases and construction loans of all banks have declined from their recent highs of 9.4% and 11.3% at the end of September of last year and 4.7% at the end of October, respectively, to 4.9%, 6.3% and 0.7% at the end of August of this year. The concentration of real estate lending of all banks declined from last year's high of 37.61% to 36.71% at the end of August of this year.

(5) The Bank has initiated the redesign of New Taiwan dollar banknotes

1. Banknote redesigns in major countries are mainly intended to strengthen anti-counterfeiting measures. Our country's current banknotes have been in use for approximately 24 years, and it is necessary to upgrade their anti-counterfeiting functions in a timely manner. More intuitive and more noticeable advanced security and anti-counterfeiting features with rapid changes and color-switching effects will be adopted. The new banknotes will take account of international developments in “green banknotes,” use more environmentally friendly raw materials, and strengthen green manufacturing processes to better meet ESG sustainable development goals. Because coins have low values and are numerous, redesign would not be cost-effective, and redesign is not being considered.

II. Through the end of this year, the Fed is expected to continue cutting interest rates, the ECB to keep policy rates unchanged, the BoJ is likely to raise interest rates, and the People's Bank of China to maintain its accommodative stance

Since April this year, in view of continuing uncertainty surrounding U.S. trade policy and its effects, the Federal Reserve System (Fed) kept its policy rate unchanged at its May, June, and July meetings. In September, it lowered its policy rate by 0.25 percentage points because of increased downside risks to employment conditions, leading the market to expect that the Fed would continue cutting interest rates. In addition, Fed Chair Powell stated in October that signs of gradually tightening liquidity were currently emerging, and that the reduction of the balance sheet might stop in the coming months. The European Central Bank (ECB) cut interest rates in both April and June this year, and kept its policy rates unchanged in July and September. Given that inflation has reached the Bank's 2% target and economic activity has picked up, policy rates are expected to remain unchanged through the end of the year.

Since April this year, considering that uncertainty about the effects of tariffs on the economy and prices remained high, the Bank of Japan (BoJ) decided to keep its policy rate unchanged in May, June, July, and September. However, BoJ Governor Ueda has stated on several occasions that if tariff uncertainty declines and the virtuous cycle of wage growth and inflation is in line with expectations, the Bank will maintain its stance of raising interest rates. In addition, in September it decided to sell its holdings of exchange-traded funds (ETFs) and Japanese real estate investment trusts (J-REITs) in the market after the necessary preparations have been completed. With economic growth in mainland China slowing and inflation remaining subdued, the People's Bank of China (hereinafter referred to as the PBoC) cut interest rates and reserve requirement ratios in May, and used a variety of policy tools to promote a recovery in the economy and prices. Monetary policy is expected to remain accommodative.

IV. The global economic outlook faces many uncertainties

Global economic growth and inflation are slowing this year. The economic outlook faces many uncertainties, including continuing uncertainty over developments in US trade policy and their effects, diverging monetary policy directions among major central banks and rising fiscal risks, risks of excessive optimism about AI and climate change, and persistent weak domestic demand and overcapacity in mainland China. Continued attention to subsequent developments and their effects is still needed.

Developments in US trade policy and their effects remain uncertain
⚫ Details of US tariff policy are not yet clear, and reciprocal tariffs and fentanyl tariffs face domestic disputes over their legality in the United States. If they are ruled unconstitutional or beyond the scope of authority in the future, this could trigger disputes and add uncertainty to the global trading system.
⚫ Recently, mainland China introduced rare-earth export controls, and the United States threatened to impose additional high tariffs on mainland China. The United States and China have also imposed maritime port fees on each other. The renewed escalation of the trade war has affected markets; the effectiveness of US tariff implementation and the direction of policy remain highly uncertain.

Diverging monetary policy directions among major central banks and rising fiscal risks
⚫ Major central banks are adjusting the direction of monetary policy, with divergent stances. This will influence future international capital flows, thereby affecting the stability of global stock, foreign exchange and bond markets.
⚫ Recent political instability in France and a downgrade of its sovereign credit rating have raised concerns across various sectors about whether a European sovereign debt crisis will recur; rapid changes in global political and economic conditions could affect the global economy and international financial stability.

Risks of excessive optimism about the AI industry and climate change
⚫ Market optimism about the AI industry has fueled a technology-centered investment boom and pushed up share prices. If subsequent productivity and profitability fall short of expectations, this could trigger substantial financial market volatility, thereby dragging down overall economic growth.
⚫ The world faces future risks of extreme weather events under climate change, which could exacerbate uncertainty about global economic growth and inflation.

Weak domestic demand and overcapacity in mainland China persist
⚫ Domestic demand in mainland China is weak and consumer confidence is insufficient, while deflationary pressures persist in some regions and industries. The stimulative effects of fiscal expansion and monetary easing policies have yet to materialize, and economic recovery remains challenging.
⚫ Overcapacity in mainland China remains severe. If surplus goods are exported in large quantities, this could increase deflationary pressures in other economies and affect related industries, exacerbating risks of trade friction and protectionism.

V. The Bank launches the redesign of New Taiwan dollar banknotes

(I) Even as electronic payments become increasingly widespread, cash remains very important
1. In most countries, cash and electronic payments show a trend of simultaneous growth
Despite the rapid development of electronic payments in recent years, actual observation of currency in circulation in major countries shows that most still exhibit a growth trend, as does our country, indicating that cash remains important. Some countries where electronic payments are widespread even ensure the status of cash through agreements or legislation, and protect the public's rights and interests in using cash.

2. Cash is irreplaceable in protecting privacy and vulnerable people, and in responding to extreme situations
Cash transactions do not require records to be left, giving cash a unique advantage in protecting privacy [隱私]. Its characteristic of not needing to rely on electronic devices or network connections makes it the only reliable payment instrument when electronic payment services are interrupted; in terms of financial inclusion, for the small number of vulnerable groups unable to obtain financial services, cash is the only payment instrument.

3. Central bank digital currency (CBDC) will not replace cash
As electronic payments are diverse and convenient, and cash usage is in good condition, central banks in various countries are relatively cautious about issuing CBDC. At present, most are at the study and evaluation stage, and research progress on the digital New Taiwan dollar is also in step with advanced countries; moreover, the basic international principle for designing CBDC is to complement rather than replace cash. Only by accommodating both cash and diverse electronic payment instruments can a competitive, inclusive, and resilient payment system be fostered.

(II) Banknote redesign is mainly intended to strengthen anti-counterfeiting and conform to the trend of ESG sustainable development
1. The current banknotes have been in use for approximately 24 years, making it necessary to upgrade their anti-counterfeiting features at an appropriate time
(1) With technological progress, anti-counterfeiting technologies are also continually improving. In countries that have redesigned their banknotes in recent years, the main purpose has been to strengthen anti-counterfeiting, to ensure that technology stays ahead of counterfeiters and make it easier for the public to distinguish genuine banknotes from counterfeits, so as to effectively eliminate counterfeit banknotes and safeguard the credibility of the national currency and the security of the public's transactions. It is also necessary for the Bank to take a forward-looking approach and begin planning the issuance of a new series of banknotes, to keep pace with international development trends.

(2) To ensure that anti-counterfeiting technology continues to stay ahead of potential counterfeiters, many countries’ central banks redesign their banknotes at intervals of approximately 16 to 24 years. Our country’s current banknotes have now been in use for 24 years, exceeding the interval between redesigns in most countries. Accordingly, the Bank and the Central Engraving and Printing Plant began research on issues related to redesign in 2017 (when the current banknotes had been in circulation for approximately 16 years).

(III) Plans for redesigning New Taiwan dollar banknotes

1. The new banknotes will adopt dynamic security and anti-counterfeiting features that are easier to recognize
The future new banknotes will take into account redesign trends at central banks in various countries and the latest international anti-counterfeiting technologies, adopting advanced security and anti-counterfeiting features that are more intuitive and more conspicuous, with rapid changes and color-switching effects, such as dynamic color-shifting inks, foils with dynamic light-and-shadow effects, and dynamic windowed security threads. This will enable the public to quickly distinguish genuine banknotes from counterfeits, prevent counterfeit banknotes from entering circulation in the first place to a very great extent, and substantially increase the difficulty of counterfeiting, making cash transactions safer.

2. The theme selection process will incorporate a mechanism for citizen participation to build social consensus
(1) Incorporating mechanisms for citizen participation into the banknote redesign process has become an international trend in recent years. In the future process of selecting themes for the new banknotes, opinions from all sectors will be broadly solicited through the “New Taiwan Dollar Banknote Theme Advisory Committee,” and a mechanism for citizen participation will be established to increase public acceptance.
(2) The advisory committee is established for a temporary task11. Its main objective is to recommend themes for the new New Taiwan dollar banknotes and methods of citizen participation in an open and transparent manner, and to submit related recommendations for the Bank’s consideration. It will be dissolved once its stage-specific tasks are completed.

3. The material, principal ink colors and dimensions of the new banknotes will remain unchanged, and accessible design will be strengthened
To accommodate the public’s usage habits and reduce the social costs of adjusting automated banknote equipment in response to the redesign, the material, principal ink colors and dimensions of the new banknotes will remain unchanged. In addition, to improve the ability of people with visual impairments to recognize banknote denominations, the new banknotes will have enlarged denomination numerals and enhanced tactile recognition features.

(IV) Study of CBDC cross-border payments

1. Cross-border payments in the current banking system still need to be processed through intermediaries [中介機構] such as correspondent banks15, resulting in problems such as slower remittances and higher remittance fees. Therefore, the BIS and the central banks of major countries are studying experimental schemes to improve cross-border payments.

2. The BIS considers that CBDC or the interconnection of countries’ fast payment systems (FPS) may be a future solution for improving cross-border payments. However, there are currently no common international standards, and international organizations still need to formulate them for countries to follow in order to resolve interoperability issues.

3. The Bank will continue to closely monitor international developments and actively participate in exchanges at relevant meetings of international organizations, to ensure that the future cross-border payment system is aligned with international practices.
