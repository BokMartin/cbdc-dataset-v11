How to read this page

Privacy index. The mean of seven design cells: can the central bank link an online payment to a person (F1), can an intermediary (F2), are private offline payments available (F4), is there a low-identity route (F7), is identity required at onboarding (F8), is targeted re-identification possible (F9), are there programmable restrictions (F10). Less access, less identification and no re-identification mean more privacy. Nothing is imputed: a cell the documents leave open is scored 0 for the lower bound and 1 for the upper bound, so a wide bar means the documents say little, not that the design is in the middle.

Beside the index, outside the mean. The four-cell index (F1, F2, F4, F7) as the narrower variant; the lawful access route (F3) as a category; the PET profile; and how many of the seven cells the competent body has decided or runs (committed, one rule for every count).

Talk, beside the index. Own-voice sentences on users' privacy per 100 eligible sentence units of the project's evidence texts; the share of those sentences that state a mechanism or a decision; and the directional gap: of the six aspects with a matching design cell, how many the project talks about without a commitment. Talk never enters the index.

The hypothesis was fixed before the data. Much talk: own-voice privacy talk in at least two publications and at least 1 relevant unit per 100. Few commitments: fewer than 2 of F1, F2, F4 and F7 committed. It holds if at least 9 of the 16 core projects meet both even at the reading least favourable to it (L), fails if at most 8 meet both at the most favourable reading (U), and is inconclusive otherwise. There is no significance test.
