## 2. What the paper is (short; details in `repo/PROJECT_BRIEF.md`, `repo/HANDOFF.md`)

A measurement-and-data article for *Data & Policy*: an open, auditable dataset of how the bodies that design retail CBDCs document user privacy, for a frame of 44 projects (43 national + the digital euro). Two layers that are never mixed: **talk** (sentence units of official documents, labelled for relevance, voice, aspect, stance, basis, decision, implementation, modality; `repo/schema/talk_units_v13_DRAFT.json`, ADR-004) and **commitments** (feature cells F1 to F12 per project from located quotes, under rules A to C of `repo/docs/MEASUREMENT_CONTRACT_v12_DRAFT.md`, which is still a draft). Since 21 Nov (ADR-009, DS has not confirmed) the headline result is a **two-axis privacy index** per project: axis 1 = stated design over eight cells (F1, F2, F3, F4, F7, F8, F9, F10) as an interval, axis 2 = commitment profile. MB's expectation, to be registered as a hypothesis: much talk, few commitments.

Corpus state (generated block of `repo/PROJECT_BRIEF.md`, corrected by PR 8 which is in the snapshot): 218 evidence publications, 207 documents with English text for 42 of 44 projects (Chile and Czechia have none), 30 of them machine-translated by gpt-6-astra, 171,865 units, 19,570 candidates. Tiers by number of evidence publications: T1 (8+) 8 projects, T2 (5 to 7) 8 projects, T3 28 projects. Human coding: relevance coded blind by both authors on 120 development units (alpha 0.46), jointly adjudicated on 22 Nov after reading a model's proposal (ADR-011, `repo/EXPOSURE_LEDGER.md` last row).

**Known lag in the snapshot.** `repo/HANDOFF.md`, `repo/PROJECT_BRIEF.md` and `repo/docs/audits/README.md` do not yet reflect ADR-011 (adjudication of 22 Nov) and the codebook v0.4 / relevance prompt v0.3 merged the same day: HANDOFF §4 still lists the disagreement workbook as waiting, HANDOFF §5 and §6 treat adjudication and codebook repair as future steps, brief step 9 says the translation judgements are not adjudicated, and the ADR list stops at ADR-010. Where they disagree with ADR-011, ADR-011 and the merged codebook are the current state; an alignment is in an open pull request (PR 12) that changes no decision. Report only contradictions beyond this lag.

## 4. What the assistant itself thinks may be wrong with these decisions

These are admissions, not conclusions. Check each; some may be wrong, and there will be problems we did not see.

1. **Process.** All proposals came from the assistant that designed the pipeline, and MB accepted almost all of them in under an hour, without DS. MB's own additions were: only 16 projects are coded blind (D1), PET under or beside F7 with the Nijsse and Pinto taxonomy (D4f), widening the axis rather than moving F10 out (D4c), no translation work (D6), 80 rather than 80 to 120 sentences (D8).
2. **D1 + D2: what "blind" covers.** Blind cells will exist for 16 projects, of which agreement is reported on 14. For the 28 T3 projects every cell comes from three-model unanimity or from the authors deciding **after** reading the model outputs. "Index from blind cells only" therefore covers 16 projects at most. Is the 16/28 split defensible, and does the labelling "exploratory" for T3 make it so?
3. **D2: unanimity of three LLMs.** Errors of the three models are likely correlated (same prompt, written by one of the vendors' models; same English text, including translations by one of the three). Unanimity may be frequent and wrong in the same direction. The unanimity rate is unknown, so the author workload of "otherwise the authors decide" is unknown.
4. **D3: the main output rests on facets nobody codes blind.** Volume (i) uses relevance (alpha 0.46 before adjudication) and voice; substance (ii) uses basis and decision; the directional gap (iv) uses stance and aspect. D8 covers only voice and aspect. ADR-006 A validates the other facets by verification of pre-filled labels, which A4 (finding F01) said does not establish validity.
5. **D3: volume in a selected corpus.** Documents enter the corpus because they bear on a cell or a privacy statement type (rule D1 rule 3, "Content"). Privacy talk per unit of document size is partly a product of that selection, and evidence publications per project range from 1 to about 15. D12 forbids the share-of-communication claim; does it also undermine "much talk" as a comparative finding across projects or tiers?
6. **D4a vs contract §7.2.** The contract says supersession happens only through a later decision of the competent body, and a general speech never overrides a specific rule. "A newer document takes precedence" may contradict that. The order between "highest status" and "newest" is not stated for the case of a newer considered claim against an older endorsed one.
7. **D4c: the construct follows the component.** The axis definition is widened so that F10 (programmable restrictions) fits, after the mock dry run showed which cells move the result. A referee may call this definition-shopping, and "control over the payment" is broader than identifiability, which the rest of the index measures.
8. **D4e: direction of F3.** F3's values are routes (via_PSP, via_CB, via_both, none_stated). Scoring via_PSP as protective assumes access through a PSP is more private than through the central bank; none_stated (a value under the contract) is undetermined in the index (`derive_index.py`). The contract still calls F3 "descriptive only".
9. **D4f: PET from one preprint.** The code list comes from one arXiv paper (v2, July 2026); the assistant does not know whether it is peer-reviewed. The same paper's Table 2 rates 20 CBDCs on privacy, 14 of them in this frame, and **both authors have read it** (MB 13:22, `02_...verbatim.md`, point 15). Comparing the index with that table can therefore not be presented as an independent external validation of blind-coded cells, and the table is exposure for 14 projects before blind coding (S7, S8). PET values may come from wholesale documents (D5) while cell values may not: one project profile, two evidence rules.
10. **D5** conflicts with rule D1 rules 1 and 2, contract §2 and AGENTS.md ("BIS ... never are" evidence) until those are rewritten; LEANING is not a status value of the contract.
11. **D6.** Taiwan (T1, 9 documents) rests entirely on machine translation from Chinese, a language the benchmark does not cover (Norwegian 3, Japanese 1, Russian 1). Contract §9 is reversed. The translation adjudication (ADR-011, lenient rule) was made after the authors read a pre-filled proposal by gpt-6-astra, the model that made the translations.
12. **D8 vs protocol v2 §4.** The protocol says: if relevance, voice or aspect fails the threshold, the unit instrument is not used in the paper. D8 replaces that with joint adjudication, so the threshold no longer has a consequence. Relevance already failed it (0.46 < 0.667). Is 80 sentences enough for an aspect with 10 categories?
13. **D9 vs rule E.** Contract §6 (F7): F7 = no requires an explicit requirement that *all* routes verify identity. Rejecting a "fully anonymous CBDC" does not exclude a low-identity tier up to a limit. Recording such a rejection as F7 = no endorsed may contradict rule E. The talk schema already has the flags `anonymity_rejected` and `low_id_tier`.
14. **D10.** MB's labels (none / considered / endorsed; none / pilot / operational) differ from rule B's values (decision: considered, endorsed, rejected, superseded, unclear; implementation: untested_or_unreported, tested, operational, unclear). "Pilot" vs "tested" and the place of rejected and superseded are not settled.
15. **Order.** HANDOFF §6 (agreed order) has no step for blind F-cell coding; the check's section 7 proposed one. D1 accepts registration first and blind locking before verification, but no single written order exists yet.
16. **D13 to D18 were accepted in one line** for seven proposals. D14 keeps translations made only because those projects were T2 on 10 Nov, so T3 coverage depends on a past tier, not on a rule. D17 leaves the literature-driven additions uneven across projects (EU, JP, CH, UK only). D18: MB wants the smallest defensible reference sample; nobody has computed what size the planned estimates (accuracy per facet value, with inclusion-probability weights) need.
17. Nothing in D1 to D18 has been run on data. The mock data still lack the design-to-talk coupling that V3 found missing.

## 5. Money and time at stake

The decisions remove the paid Chinese benchmark (D6). They add author hours: 16 projects × 8 cells + PET, coded blind by two authors (index spec §10.3 estimated tens of hours per author for 44 projects × 4 cells), 80 sentences of voice and aspect each (about 1 to 2 hours), adjudication of non-unanimous model cells for 44 projects. Paid runs (three models, full scope) were estimated on 17 Nov at several hundred USD (HANDOFF §8, estimates, not measurements). A design error found after production costs a full re-run.
